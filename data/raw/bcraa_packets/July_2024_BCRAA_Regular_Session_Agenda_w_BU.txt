SUBJECT:         Budget Transfer 432-433 BCRAA

RECOMMENDATION FOR BOARD ACTION:
Approve and adopt a budget transfer in the amount of $8,262 for the procurement of a Baumalight Mulcher MP360.

DISCUSSION/SUMMARY:
Airport staff is in the process of procuring a Baumalight Mulcher for use at all three airfields administered by the Authority.
In the past, the Authority has never had the equipment necessary to to reduce brush, shrubs, small trees at all three
airfields. This was all done by hand, involving outside assistance from Cal-Fire - Alder Camp work crews and involved
multiple days, if not weeks, to get done. This required not only budgeting for their services, but also waiting on their
availability. In the summer, with fires a real threat, the availability of work crews in the summer and fall months, is almost
nonexistent. By procuring our own equipment, we can maintain our airfields in a quick and efficient manner, saving time
and labor to ensure we meet FAA and Cal-Trans requirement and standards.

The total purchase price after taxes is $12,390.30. The Authority received $48,000 this FY due to the Smith River
Complex and the required Emergency Land Use Agreement with the USDA for their exclusive use of Ward Field in
Gasquet, CA. Due to these unexpected revenue received and the unused funds in the lines outlined below, there are
sufficient funds available for the procurement of the Baumalight Mulcher.

Due to this piece of equipment being utilized at all three airfields, the total purchase price will be split between:

 FROM:          Ryan Cooley, Airport Director
 SUBJECT:       FY 24/25 BCRAA Budget

 RECOMMENDATION FOR BOARD ACTION:
 Review and approve the updated FY 24-25 BCRAA budget, approved by the Del Norte County Board of
 Supervisors and direct staff to submit the adopted budget to the Del Norte County Auditor-Controller. **

 SUMMARY/DISCUSSION:
 BCRAA administers airport operations at three public use airports on behalf of Del Norte County as a Joint
 Powers Authority (JPA) and is responsible for three budget units (432-433: BCRAA; 433-434: Special
 Aviation; and 142-323: Airport/PFC) related to operating the County’s airports. Under the terms of the JPA
 agreement BCRAA’s Board of Commissioners is responsible for adopting an annual operating budget for
 each of the budget units under its management. Additionally, pursuant to Article IV, Section 4.3 (“Approval of
 Budget”) of the JPA agreement, the budget adopted by BCRAA must also be approved or ratified by any
 member of the JPA that contributes funds to the JPA. As Del Norte County is presently the only member of
 the JPA contributing funds to BCRAA, the County must also approve or ratify the BCRAA budget before it
 can be considered final.

 The proposed BCRAA budget (432-433) is included in its entirety as an attachment to this Board Report for
 the Board’s consideration (see column “24/25 Dept” for proposed budget lines). In general, the budget
 proposed is similar to the budgets adopted in the past with respect to normal sources of operating revenue
 and expense. Normal operating revenue (i.e. revenue generated from our leases with Advanced Air, the
 TSA, FedEx, Cal-Ore Life Flight, hangar tenants, etc.) is stable as well as the car rental concession revenue
 with Avis and Budget. Anticipated revenue from increased air carrier operations including passenger facility
 charges, fuel flowage fees and landing fees as well as minor reductions in services and supplies helped to
 prepare a balanced budget without the need to draw from the general fund.

The BCRAA Board has adopted a budget including a full annual payment on the Caltrans loan ($21,055).
Salaries and benefits calculations include staffing for all four allocated positions for the full year with regard to
payroll ($325,380, includes 5% annual step increases for effected employees and a 2% increase for
unexpected increases such as potential COLA adjustments this FY), overtime ($5,000), retirement ($54,148)
and employee benefits ($56,387) as well as the CalPERS retirement unfunded accrued liability amount
($62,975). Other normal expenses related to services provided by the County have been calculated and
entered by the County including the Cost Plan allocation (68,314, an increase of $10,657 from $57,657 the
year prior), computer leases ($9,813 an increase of $8,363 from $1,450 the year prior), property ($62,040, a
decrease of $1,711 from $63,751 the year prior) and liability insurance ($10,200 an increase of $1,194 from
$9,006 from the year prior), and the Worker’s Compensation Insurance amount ($3,499 a decrease of $296
from $3,795 the year prior), etc.
Nonstandard expenditures to note for the FY 24-25 budget include an increase in the following line items:

     •   Professional Services – Legal ($100,000) for ongoing litigation
     •   Professional Services ($25,000) for the ongoing legal engineering consultant services
     •   Equipment ($12,000) for Secondary Wind Direction Indicator replacement and instillation and ($8,000)
         for new Ground Power Unit Transformer on the Commercial Apron.

 The FY 24-25 budget being presented is a balanced budget.

 The Del Norte County Board of Supervisors approved the 24/25 budget at its regular session meeting on
 June 6 and the Budget is now being presented to the BCRAA Board for final approval.                            6

Summary
By this Order, the U.S. Department of Transportation (the Department) approves the application
submitted by the Border Coast Regional Airport Authority (BCRAA or Applicant) for the
community of Crescent City, California (Crescent City) under the Alternate Essential Air Service
(AEAS) program. The Department will enter into a grant agreement with BCRAA on behalf of
Crescent City to provide up to $19,312,532 for the four year term from October 1, 2024, through
September 30, 2028, payable in an amount not to exceed $4,161,287 in year one (October 1,
2024, through September 30, 2025), $4,577,415 in year two (October 1, 2025, through
September 30, 2026), $5,035,157 in year three (October 1, 2026, through September 30, 2027),
and $5,538,673 in year four (October 1, 2027, through September 30, 2028). Crescent City
intends to use the grant to secure public charter service provided by Advanced Air, LLC
(Advanced Air), using 30-seat Dornier 328 (Dornier 328) jet aircraft, from Del Norte County
Regional Airport (CEC) to Oakland International Airport (OAK) and Hawthorne Municipal
Airport (HHR) or an alternate hub airport designated by the community.

Background on Alternate Essential Air Service
Congress established AEAS to provide communities with an alternative to the traditional EAS-
type service. 2 Typically, the EAS program pays subsidy to regional air carriers to provide a
minimum level of air service to a major hub airport with twin-engine aircraft. Under the AEAS
program, the Department may provide financial assistance directly to the eligible community to

Background
By Order 2020-9-2, the Department approved an application submitted in 2020 by BCRAA on
behalf of the community of Crescent City under the AEAS program. The Department entered
into a grant agreement with BCRAA on behalf of Crescent City to provide up to $14,692,683
during the four-year term from October 1, 2020, through September 30, 2024. Crescent City
used the grant for public charter service from CEC to OAK, with service provided by Corporate
Flight Management, Inc. d/b/a Contour Airlines, Inc. (Contour) using 30-seat Embraer ERJ-135
(EMB-135) aircraft. CEC changed air service providers, as permitted by the grant agreement, to
Advanced Air effective March 17, 2024, to continue providing public charter service from CEC

to OAK and HHR through September 30, 2024, using Dornier 328 jet aircraft.

Crescent City’s Alternate Essential Air Service Application
On February 22, 2024, BCRAA, on behalf of Crescent City, submitted an application for
financial assistance under the AEAS program. 4 Crescent City requests a new four-year AEAS
grant, to provide up to $19,312,532 for the four year term from October 1, 2024, through
September 30, 2028, payable in an amount not to exceed $4,161,287 in year one (October 1,
2024, through September 30, 2025), $4,577,415 in year two (October 1, 2025, through
September 30, 2026), $5,035,157 in year three (October 1, 2026, through September 30, 2027),
and $5,538,673 in year four (October 1, 2027, through September 30, 2028). The application
states CEC performed an extensive analysis of other EAS communities between May 1, 2018,
and October 2023, and found that EAS subsidy levels were approximately 71.8 percent higher in
2023 than in 2018, while Crescent City’s subsidy levels have increased only 14.3 percent during
the same time period. The application further states that, in order to increase flight frequencies
to 12 round trips per week, subsidy amounts for CEC’s new grant term must reflect the increased
costs of flying, which will support CEC’s goal of returning to consistent historical passenger
enplanement levels. In working with Advanced Air to negotiate pricing for such service,
BCRAA determined that the amounts requested in their application will provide adequate
financial support for AEAS.

Decision
The Department has decided, pending execution of a grant agreement, to approve Crescent
City’s application for continued acceptance in the AEAS program, for a new, four-year term.

Reminder About EAS Eligibility
To remain eligible for EAS, communities must comply with all applicable EAS eligibility
requirements. We note that many of the traditional eligibility requirements, including those
below, do not apply for Fiscal Year 2024, pursuant to the Consolidated Appropriations Act,
2024, Pub. L. No. 118-42 (March 9, 2024), 138 Stat 25, 303.

Title 49 U.S.C. § 41731(a)(1)(B) provides that a community must maintain an average of 10
enplanements per service day, as determined by the Secretary of Transportation, during the most
recent fiscal year, to remain eligible for EAS. Communities, such as Crescent City, that are
more than 175 driving miles from the nearest large or medium hub airport, are exempt from this
requirement under 49 U.S.C. § 41731(d).

RE: Border Coast Regional Airport Authority – Alternate Essential Air Service Grant Award

Crescent City, CA – June 27, 2024 – The Border Coast Regional Airport Authority has secured a new
4-year AEAS grant agreement with the DOT. The agreement ensures continued, expanded
connectivity for Crescent City, CA and the surrounding Border Coast Region. Advanced Air, LLC
offers daily round-trip service from Crescent City (CEC) to Oakland (OAK) alongside new twice-
weekly round-trip flights between Crescent City (CEC) and Hawthorne/Los Angeles (HHR).

Since March 2024, multiple collaborative meetings involving The Border Coast Regional Airport
Authority, Mr. Michael Mooney of Volaire Aviation Consulting, Advanced Air, LLC, and the
Department of Transportation have taken place. The primary objective has been to enhance regional
air service ensuring safety, reliability and expanding destination options to boost ridership, stimulate
business growth and promote tourism.

Crucial to these efforts is increased funding from the DOT for Alternate Essential Air Service,
supporting economic development and opportunity in our region. The DOT has awarded a new four-
year AEAS grant totaling $19,312,532, a $4.6 million increase from the previous agreement. This
funding boost will continue to facilitate existing flights to both Oakland (OAK) and Hawthorne/Los
Angeles (HHR), with increased frequency starting Summer of 2025.

This initiative is part of the Department of Transportation's (DOT) Alternate Essential Air Service
(AEAS) Program, originally granted in 2018. The existing agreement is valid through September 30,
2024. The AEAS program ensures continued air service for small communities across the United
States originally served by certificated air carriers pre-1978 deregulation, through subsidies for air
service.

The Border Coast Regional Airport Authority is excited to continue to provide enhanced air service
that will benefit our community and foster regional growth. These developments underscore our
commitment to providing reliable transportation options and advancing economic opportunities for the
Border Coast Region.

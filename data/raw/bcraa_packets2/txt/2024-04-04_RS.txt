SUBJECT: Airport Staffing Update – Position Title Change

RECOMMENDATION FOR BOARD ACTION:
Review and approved proposed revisions to the current Airport Staffing Chart, Job Description and
title change, from Airport Fiscal & Program Manager II to Assistant Airport Director, and authorize the
Airport Director to submit the revised staffing chart, job description and title change to the Del Norte
County Human Resources Division.

SUMMARY:
Staff is requesting approval of a change in the staffing chart, modification to the job description and
title change from the position of Airport Fiscal & Program Manager II to Assistant Airport Director
in order to allow for a more accurate representation of the duties and responsibilities the current
position entails, without fiscal impact to the annual BCRAA budget.

DISCUSSION:
The staff of the Border Coast Regional Airport Authority (BCRAA) are classified as “conditional”
employees of Del Norte County who are essentially provided by the County to conduct airport
management and airport operations activities of the County’s airports with reimbursement for the
costs associated with the airport staffing (i.e. payroll, benefits, retirement, etc.) provided by BCRAA to
the County. That being said, the MOU addressing the administration of BCRAA and the services to
be provided by the County between BCRAA and the County makes specific provisions stating that
decisions involving the airport staffing are to remain solely under the discretion of BCRAA (See
attached, DN CO AGMT #2010-022).

Over the years, the airport management duties and responsibilities have become increasingly
complex particularly under the various programs that the airport has significant involvement with,
notably the requirement of the airport’s Part 139 certification, the provisioning of Airport Rescue and
Fire Fighting during commercial operations, the participation in the FAA’s Airport Improvement
Program, the Part 1542 Airport Security Program, and various other regulatory requirements. While
the duties expected to be performed by the Airport Fiscal & Program Manager have grown
exponentially more demanding and complex as a result of the evolving landscape of airport
management, the position has outgrown the outline of the original position of Airport Fiscal & Program
Manager.
The position of Fiscal & Program Manager has continued to grow and become an increasingly
complex position in support of the Airport Director, specifically in administering the Disadvantaged
Business Enterprise program, the Passenger Facility Charge program, AIP compliance, day-to-day
fiscal management including audit preparation, preparation of Board agendas and minutes, various
airfield duties and airport security program implementation, and a number of other miscellaneous
duties. The previous Airport Director in their memo to the board on April 1, 2021, stated that the
Fiscal & Program Manager functions “as a de facto assistant department head, particularly during
times when the Airport Director is unavailable.”

In light of the increasing demands and responsibilities related to airport administration, as well as to
ensure we retain qualified staff, I am recommending that the BCRAA staff position of Airport Fiscal &
Program Manager II be changed to Assistant Airport Director. The corresponding job description has
been revised accordingly and are attached, as exhibits, to this Board Report.

As an organization we rely exclusively on the staff to advance our mission. After careful consideration
it is my recommendation to revise the title and job description in order to better align with the
substantial responsibilities of the position.

ALTERNATIVES:
Do not approve the title change and update to job description.

Description of Basic Function
Serving as staff to the Border Coast Regional Airport Authority, this position provides administrative
services related to various airport programs as well as day-to-day management of the Authority’s
fiscal programs and airport operations under the direction of the Airport Director. This is a responsible
administrative mid- management level position that requires the accounting skills and experience to
perform a variety of complex and technical budgetary and program duties as well as a the skills and
experience to assist in the oversight of airport operations and security, under indirect supervision and
the ability to demonstrate independence and proficiency in handling complex and difficult fiscal and
administrative assignments unique to airports, including administration of the airport in the absence of
the Airport Director.

Essential Duties
Essential job duties may include any of the following tasks, knowledge, skills and other
characteristics. The duties stated here are not provided as a comprehensive list, rather the duties
stated here are a representative summary of the major duties and responsibilities. Incumbents may
not be required to perform all duties listed and may be required to perform additional, position
relevant, tasks as necessary.

DATE: April 1, 2024                       AGENDA DATE:             April 4, 2024
 TO:            Border Coast Regional Airport Authority Board of Commissioners
 FROM:          Ryan Cooley, Airport Director

 SUBJECT:       Overview of AAAE/ACI-NA Washington D.C. Legislative Conference


SUMMARY/DISCUSSION:
This two-day conference provided the Airport Director with the opportunity to hear directly from members of Congress,
administration officials, and Capitol Hill staff about what the airport industry can expect from Washington in the year
ahead. There were plenty of group discussions held, speakers addressing various aspects of the airport landscape and
the challenges airports are facing, both large and small.

    •    FAA FY 2024 Budget - While the House and Senate versions of the FAA reauthorization bill have a lot of
         commonalities, FAA has concerns over language under consideration that would increase the allocation of AIP
         entitlement dollars to airports and the impact proposed formula changes could have on discretionary funding.
         FAA is hopeful to be fully funded for FY 2024 by this summer at the latest. Large FY2024 AIP Projects are
         dependent on a full FY 2024 budget approval.

    •    Environmental and PFAS - PFAS (per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances) in firefighting foam and the
         conversations around PFAS will continue to evolve for years as the FAA determines the difference between
         PFAS in firefighting foam and other potential PFAS sources at the airport. Two new F3 foams have been
         approved by the FAA for use on airports for ARFF purposes, but they do not perform in the same way as
         traditional foam. Training will be critical on how to apply new foam if/when airports switch. The FAA is aware of
         the large cost to airports for removal and disposal of unused foam which contains PFAS, as well as the potential
         need to replace equipment in the future that has been contaminated with PFAS that can not be cleaned. The
         FAA is currently working through the process on how this will all transpire in the future. Airports are still able to
         utilize existing approved firefighting foams. There is no mandate to switch at this time.

    •    Aviation Fuel Tax – I used my limited time for questions to inquire about the Aviation Fuel Tax which California
         continues to divert for nonairport purposes. As of the December 13, 2023 report, the U.S. Department of
         Transportation Office of Inspector General still has California listed as non-compliant in regards to proper use of
         Aviation Fuel Tax. Mr. Elliott Black of the FAA Compliance Program pulled me aside immediately after my
         question and informed me that he would look into this further, as he was unaware that California continues to
         divert Aviation Fuel Tax for nonairport purposes.

    •    Meeting With Congressman Huffman - The office of Congressman Huffman had to cancel at the last minute,
         so a virtual meeting was held this past Monday instead. Items discussed included Aviation Fuel Tax, the lack of
         CalTrans airport funding for our small, non NPIAS (National Plan of Integrated Airport Systems) airfields which
         are not federally funded by the FAA, and the potential for state funding to assist with local 5% matches.

 SUBJECT: Tolowa Dee-Ni’ Nation Land Acknowledgement


INTRODUCTION:
Review, consider and provide guidance on proposed Tolowa Dee-ni’ Nation Land Acknowledgement.**

SUMMARY/DISCUSSION:
In light of the fact that the Del Norte County Regional Airport is located on the ancestral homelands of the Tolowa people,
Mrs. Emily Reed – Public Relations Manager for the Tolowa Dee-ni’ Nation has inquired about the possibility and
willingness of the Border Coast Regional Airport Authority to officially acknowledge the location. This may be
accomplished by creating and placing a plaque in the passenger terminal or a create and display a permanent declaration
on the electronic displays throughout the passenger terminal, as well as on the official flycrescentcity.com website.

Sample verbiage:

“The Border Coast Regional Airport Authority hereby recognizes that the Del Norte County Regional Airport is located on
the ancestral homelands of the Tolowa people, who have stewarded this land throughout the generations. We
acknowledge that this land has a traditional name, Taa-ghii~-'a~ located in the Taa-'at-dvn district. The Border Coast
Regional Airport Authority recognizes and respects the enduring relationship that has existed between the many
Indigenous peoples and their homelands since time immemorial.”

ALTERNATIVES:
Do not move forward with a formal land acknowledgement
Provide airport staff with alternative direction

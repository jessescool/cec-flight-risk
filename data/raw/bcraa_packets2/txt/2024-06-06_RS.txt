SUBJECT:        Clothing Resolution – 2024-001

RECOMMENDATION FOR BOARD ACTION:
Review, consider and approve resolution 2024-001 regarding Airport Operations & Maintenance clothing benefits.**

DISCUSSION/SUMMARY:
Resolution 2019-02 adopted the current Memorandum of Understanding between DNCEA/SEIU Local 1021 and the
County of Del Norte, and all subsequent agreements between the bargaining unit and the County, governing the benefits
of the Authority for all employee positions, including clothing allowances.

In the past the Authority has budgeted $500 each year for clothing purchases. Items purchased in the past include rain
gear, jackets, ARFF hooded sweatshirts and work boots. This year CEC branded work hoodies were in the process of
being ordered due to the existing hoodies provided years ago, are wearing out. However, Article IX, section 9.14 of the
Memorandum of Understanding between DNCEA/SEIU Local 1021 only outlines clothing benefits to cover employee
groups listed within the MOU specifically. As such, since BCRAA employees are not listed specifically, the Del Norte
County Auditor-Controller is requiring a resolution which names the specific BCRAA employee groups which can benefit
from the clothing allowance budgeted for each year.

ALTERNATIVES:
Do not approve clothing benefits for BCRAA Employees.

    A RESOLUTION OF THE BOARD OF COMMISSIONERS OF THE BORDER COAST

      DESIGNATING THE AIRPORT MANAGER AS PURCHASING AGENT FOR THE
          AUTHORITY AND ESTABLISHING THE POWERS OF THAT OFFICE

WHEREAS, the Border Coast Regional Airport Authority (the Authority) is a public entity
established under Government Code § 6500 et seq., the "Joint Powers Authority Law";
and

WHEREAS, the Joint Powers Agreement of the Authority provides that the Board may adopt
rules and regulations for the conduct of its affairs; and

WHEREAS, the Agreement further provides that the “manner of exercising the powers of the
Authority” shall be that governing the exercise of like powers by Del Norte County; and

WHEREAS, the work conditions of the Authority’s employees warrant that they be afforded
certain clothing allowances comparable to Del Norte County employees; and

WHEREAS, the Board of Commissioners previously adopted Resolution 2019-02 adopting the
current Memorandum of Understanding between DNCEA/SEIU Local 1021, the Del Norte
County Mid Management Bargaining Unit, the Del Norte County Department Head Bargaining
Unit and the County of Del Norte as well as all subsequent agreements between these
bargaining units and the County, governing the benefits of the Authority for all employee
positions; and

WHEREAS, Article IX, section 9.14 of the Memorandum of Understanding between
DNCEA/SEIU Local 1021 outlines clothing benefits to covered employee groups listed in the
MOU, including providing clothing/uniforms to specified employees to assist the public in readily
identifying employees acting in an official capacity and/or where normal work duties expose the
employee's clothing to contamination, excessive soiling or excessive wear and tear but does not
list BCRAA Airport Employees

NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVED that the Board of Commissioners of the Border
Coast Regional Airport Authority hereby finds, determines, and resolves as follows:

   1. Airport Operations & Maintenance Employees shall be allowed protective footwear, one
      (1) hooded sweatshirt, and one (1) jacket every other year.

SUBJECT:         AIP 48 – JPA Contribution Line-Item Creation

RECOMMENDATION FOR BOARD ACTION:
Consider and approve the creation of fund balance line(s) specific to JPA member contributions towards the upcoming AIP-48
Runway 18-36 Rehab Project, to only be used for said project, and direct staff to request the Del Norte County Auditor-Controller
create specific line-item number(s) necessary for the allocation of JPA contributions specific to the project..**

DISCUSSION/SUMMARY:
Based on direction received from the board at the January 12, 2023 special session, airport staff held discussions with the
JPA members’ administrators to solicit financial contributions to be used to help fund the required 5% local match for the
upcoming AIP 48 runway rehabilitation project. Total contributions pledged are as follows:

Del Norte County – Del Norte County will contribute $77,000 towards the 5% match with a caveat for increasing funding
based on other members contributions (Recently advised this will be available FY 24-25 per CAO office).

The City of Crescent City – Crescent City Council will contribute $77,000 towards the 5% match.

Elk Valley Rancheria – Elk Valley Rancheria will contribute $77,000 towards the 5% match.

The City of Brookings – The City of Brookings will contribute $10,000 per year for three (3) years starting FY 23-24
towards the 5% match.

Curry County – Curry County will provide $38,500 in FY 23-24 and FY 24-25 towards the 5% match.

Tolowa Dee-ni’ Nation – The Tolowa Dee-ni’ Nation will contribute $77,000 towards the 5% match (Recently advised this
will be split, $40,000 FY 24, $37,000 FY 25)

Total Contributions: $415,000

Invoices have been sent to each JPA member for their specific pledged amounts. In order to receive the funds in FY 23-
24 but utilize them in FY 24-25, new line(s) needs to be created specific to JPA member contributions for AIP 48.


ALTERNATIVES:
Do not approve creation of specific line.

18-36 Rehabilitation, Marking, and Lighting Upgrades project pursuant to AIP No. 3-06-0057-048-2024 on

and the concurrence of the FAA Airport District Office Program Manager, Airport staff is recommending

SUMMARY:
The Border Coast Regional Airport Authority (BCRAA) administers airport operations at three public use
airports on behalf of Del Norte County as a Joint Powers Authority (JPA) and is responsible for three
budget units (432-433: BCRAA; 433-434: Special Aviation; and 142-323: Airport/PFC) related to operating
the County’s airports. The proposed budgets are included in their entirety as attachments to this Board
Report for the Board’s consideration (see column “24/25 Dept” for proposed budget lines). In general, the
budgets proposed are similar to the budgets adopted last year with respect to normal sources of operating
revenue and expense.

DISCUSSION:
The proposed BCRAA budget (432-433) is included in its entirety as an attachment to this Board Report
for the Board’s consideration (see column “24/25 Dept” for proposed budget lines). In general, the budget
proposed is similar to the budgets adopted in the past with respect to normal sources of operating
revenue and expense. Normal operating revenue (i.e. revenue generated from our leases with Contour
Airlines, the TSA, FedEx, Cal-Ore Life Flight, hangar tenants, etc.) is stable as well as the car rental
concession revenue with Avis and Budget.

As far as specific budget lines of note, the annual contribution for the administration of the airport from Del
Norte County ($271,608) is anticipated to remain the same.

In regards to the Terminal Loan, pursuant to DN CO AGMT #2016-149 Section 5, payment is to be made
from specified sources of funds referred to as “Pledged Revenue”. This includes (a) any contributions
received by BCRAA from member entities or any other source specified for the sole purpose of this loan
repayment; (b) funds derived from a reallocation of the tax on sales of aviation fuel. In light of the fact that
“Pledged Revenue” has not been received, a payment for the loan provided by Del Norte County for the
terminal project will not be made. The full annual payment on the Caltrans loan ($21,055) has been
budgeted. Salaries and benefits calculations include staffing for all four allocated positions for the full year
with regard to payroll ($325,380, includes 5% annual step increases for effected employees and a 2%
increase for unexpected increases such as potential COLA adjustments this FY), overtime ($5,000),
retirement ($54,148) and employee benefits ($56,387) as well as the CalPERS retirement unfunded
accrued liability amount ($62,975). Other normal expenses related to services provided by the County
have been calculated and entered by the County including the Cost Plan allocation (68,314, an increase
of $10,657 from $57,657 the year prior), computer leases ($9,813 an increase of $8,363 from $1,450 the
year prior), property ($62,040, a decrease of $1,711 from $63,751 the year prior) and liability insurance
($10,200 an increase of $1,194 from $9,006 from the year prior), and the Worker’s Compensation
Insurance amount ($3,499 a decrease of $296 from $3,795 the year prior), etc.

Nonstandard expenditures to note for the FY 24-25 budget include an increase in the following line items:

   •   Professional Services – Legal ($100,000) for ongoing litigation
   •   Professional Services ($25,000) for the ongoing legal engineering consultant services
   •   Equipment ($12,000) for Secondary Wind Direction Indicator replacement and instillation and
       ($8,000) for new Ground Power Unit Transformer on the Commercial Apron.

As the Board is aware, special sources of revenue and expense, such as grants, can vary from year to
year and, as per normal, mid-year budget amendments (i.e. Budget Transfers) are necessary when grants
are secured for projects identified in the Airport Capital Improvement Plan. Currently, we have four active
AIP projects that are programmed in the budget: AIP 39 (Obstruction Removal Design Project), AIP 40
(Runway 18-36 Rehab Project, Phase I), AIP 43 (ARFF Equipment), AIP 44 (Runway 18-36 Rehab
Project, Phase II Final Design). For FY 24-25 we are anticipating bringing AIP 48 (Runway 18-36 Rehab
Project, Construction), Obstruction Removal (Phase 2 – Construction) and Fuel Farm Infrastructure
Replacement (Design) into the budget.


In the Special Aviation (433-434) budget, like last year, we are once again including an expenditure of
$1,500 in order to pay for assistance from the Del Norte County Roads Division for work necessary at
Andy McBeth Airport in Klamath and Ward Field in Gasquet such as runway surface cleaning and
transportation of our mowing tractor.

The FY 24-25 budget will be in deficit $75,790. This is a reduction of the $23,006 compared to FY 23-24
deficit of $98,796.

Under the terms of the JPA agreement BCRAA’s Board of Commissioners is responsible for adopting an
annual operating budget for each of the budget units under its management. Additionally, pursuant to the
JPA agreement, the budget adopted by BCRAA must also be ratified by any member of the JPA that

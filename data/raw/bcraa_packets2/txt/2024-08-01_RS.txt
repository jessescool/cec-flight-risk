This Agreement for Air Services (this "Agreement") is made and entered into as of October 1st, 2024 (the
"Eﬀective Date") by and between Advanced Air, LLC, a California corporation with its principal oﬃce at
12101 Crenshaw Blvd., Hawthorne, California 90250 ("Advanced Air, LLC"), and Border Coast Regional
Airport Authority, a Joint Powers Authority of the State of California, which includes California and
Oregon political subdivisions and Federally recognized sovereign Indian tribes, with its principal
administrative oﬃce at 1650 Dale Rupert Rd., Suite 100 Crescent City, CA 95531 (the "Authority").

                                                 Recitals
WHEREAS, through a Master Lease Agreement with airport owner Del Norte County, Authority is the
operator of the Del Norte County Regional Airport with its principal administrative airport oﬃce at 1650
Dale Rupert Rd., Suite 100 Crescent City, CA 95531 ("Airport"); and

WHEREAS, the Authority, under Docket Number DOT-OST-1997-2649 and Order 2024- 5-25 ("Order") and
pursuant to 49 U.S.C. § 41745(a), has been awarded an Alternative Essential Air Service Grant (AEAS) by
the U.S. Department of Transportation ("DOT") in the total amount of $19,312,532 for a four year term
from October 1, 2024 through September 30, 2028, payable in an amount not to exceed $4,161,287 in
year one (October 1, 2024, through September 30, 2025), $4,577,415 in year two (October 1, 2025,
through September 30, 2026), $5,035,157 in year three (October 1, 2026, through September 30, 2027),
and $5,538,673 in year four (October 1, 2027, through September 30, 2028) under DOT's AEAS Program
("Grant"), which the Authority will use to fund passenger air service at the Airport; and AEAS at the
Airport as permitted by 49 U.S.C. § 4l 745(a) and DOT's Order dated July 27, 2004, establishing AEAS
(docket DOT-OST-2004-18715). DOT accepted the Authority's AEAS election; and

WHEREAS Advanced Air, LLC is a licensed direct air carrier under 14 C.F.R. Parts 119, 135, and 298, and
holds all licenses, certiﬁcates, and permits from applicable governmental authorities for the conduct of
its business as an AEAS direct air carrier; and

WHEREAS, the Authority and Advanced Air, LLC intend to enter into Amendment No. 1 to the Terminal
Building Oﬃce Space Lease Agreement to be eﬀective October 1, 2024 that authorizes Advanced Air, LLC
to continue using the Airport's main passenger terminal and associated ramp space in connection with
providing AEAS through September 30, 2028 (the "Lease") and Amendment No. 1 to the Landing Permit
to be eﬀective October 1, 2024 which authorizes Advanced Air, LLC to use the Airport's facilities in
connection with providing AEAS (the "Permit") (together the Lease and the Permit are the "Related
Agreements"); and

WHEREAS the parties hereto desire to enter into this Agreement to deﬁne the terms and conditions
under which Advanced Air, LLC agrees to provide AEAS under 14 C.F.R. Pt. 380, and the Authority agrees
to pay Advanced Air, LLC for said services through and subject to the Grant in order to guarantee
Advanced Air, LLC a minimum revenue for the AEAS.

have executed an amendment to the existing Grant Agreement for DOT' s Grant award ("Grant

Related Agreements; and (iii) the Authority and Advanced Air, LLC have satisﬁed any and all conditions
precedent under the Related Agreements.

 The Authority warrants and represents that it will take all steps necessary to keep the Airport
"Federalized" as that term is used by the Transportation Security Administration ("TSA") and to have all
equipment and personnel (whether provided by the TSA or otherwise) in place and ready to screen
passengers and baggage on October 1, 2024. The parties acknowledge and agree that Advanced Air, LLC
is not obligated to perform under this Agreement unless TSA screening is in place at Del Norte County
Regional Airport, Jack McNamara Field.

 In addition, the parties understand and agree that no press releases, other media coverage (including,

entity, subject to California laws pertaining to the agenda requirements and public discussion

4. Compensation for Services.

 In accordance with the terms and conditions set forth in the Grant Agreement between Authority and
DOT, Authority agrees to pay, and Advanced Air, LLC agrees to accept, a Flight Segment Fee during each
year of the Grant period of October 1, 2024, through September 30, 2028 as set forth in Attachment A
hereto along with the payment of certain speciﬁc ﬁxed line item expenses related to Non-Completed
Flights during the Grant period of October 1, 2024, through September 30, 2028 as set forth in
Attachment A hereto ("Compensation for Services").

 Except as otherwise permitted by DOT, Authority and Advanced Air, LLC agree that planned Flight
Segments that are cancelled in advance (prior to take oﬀ) may not be compensable for reasons
including, but not limited to: temporary ﬂight restrictions, no booked passengers in an outbound and
corresponding return Flight Segments (as noted in Attachment A), mechanical issues, ATC issues, and
crew shortages/issues ( each a "Non-Completed Flight Segment"). As stated in the Grant, DOT will
consider reimbursement of certain speciﬁc ﬁxed, line-item expenses relating to Non-Completed Flight
Segments under this Agreement (e.g. for ﬁxed line-item expenses related to Non-Completed Flight
Segments arising due to airﬁeld construction that leads to an extended closure of runway(s)). Such
reimbursements must be directly related to ﬁxed auditable expenses incurred by Advanced Air, LLC
despite the Flight Segments being cancelled. Per the Grant, such reimbursements must be paid to
Advanced Air, LLC prior to being considered for reimbursement by DOT. The total of such
reimbursements for Non-Completed Flight Segment ﬁxed auditable expenses will not exceed the
applicable limit stated in Attachment A for any month during the Term.

 The Federal Aviation Administration ("FAA") has authorized the Authority to collect a Passenger Facility
Charge of $4.50 per passenger departing from the Airport ("PFC"). The Authority warrants and
represents that it has not yet collected the maximum total PFCs currently authorized by the FAA for the
Airport. Until such time as: (i) the Authority's approval to collect PFCs at the Airport is modiﬁed, revoked,
or expires; or (ii) the Authority has collected the maximum amount approved by the FAA, Advanced Air,
LLC will collect a PFC from each passenger using Advanced Air, LLC's air service who departs from the
Airport. Advanced Air, LLC will remit all passenger fees collected in each calendar month to the Authority
in full, without oﬀset or deduction, not later than the twentieth (20th) day of the calendar month
following the calendar month in which the PFCs were collected. With the exception of the PFCs,
Advanced Air, LLC will retain all revenue it earns from ticket sales and fees collected under this
Agreement.

5. Invoicing and Payment.

A. Not later than the ﬁfth (5th) business day of each calendar month, Advanced Air, LLC will invoice the
Authority for the Compensation for Services performed for the prior calendar month.

B. Within thirty (30) calendar days of Authority's receipt of said monthly invoice from Advanced Air, LLC,
the Authority will submit the Compensation for Services to Advanced Air, LLC via ACH payment as to the
bank account identiﬁed by Advanced Air, LLC to Authority from time to time in writing, in accordance
with and subject to the Grant, DOT's rules and regulations, as well as applicable state and federal law.

C. Subject to Section 4 of this Agreement, the obligations of the Authority, its agents and employees
under this Section 5 will survive the expiration or termination of this Agreement.

      o Phase 1: Project Design
             Funding approved by AIP program (AIP 39, FY19): $690,391
             Federal environmental approval (NEPA) issued with Record of
               Decision/Finding of No Significant Impact (ROD/FONSI).
             90% Original Plan Design was almost complete by GHD.
             Project is being designed by GHD due to environmental issues related to
               removal of naturally occurring obstructions (i.e. trees) that are located within
               environmentally sensitive areas within the California Coastal Zone.
             AIP 39, initiated in August 2019, aimed to remove obstructions at the approach
               end of Runway 30 at Jack McNamara Field. The original design included
               extensive environmental mitigation measures due to federal funding
               requirements. However, by the middle of 2021, it was deemed too complex and
               costly to implement. Consequently, the project was re-envisioned as a Timber
               Harvest Plan (THP).
             The project faced delays during the pandemic in 2020 and 2021. After the
               appointment of a new Airport Director the project resumed with a THP focus in
               December of 2021. Throughout 2022, airport staff collaborated with GHD and
               forestry consultant, Able Forestry, to draft a modified scope of work. In Spring
               2023, airport staff requested a Period of Performance extension from the FAA to
               allow GHD extra time to complete the revised project due to time lost because of
               the pandemic, and the complications in refocusing the project. Airport staff was
               notified by the FAA in September of 2023 that the extension request was
               received, and was subsequently approved on June 24, 2024.
             GHD submitted the updated scope of work to the FAA on July 16, 2024, but it
               was rejected on July 17, 2024. The FAA required the original mitigation
               components be included due to the NEPA documents filed in 2018. A meeting on
               July 23, 2024, revealed that federal funding is incompatible with a THP due to
               less stringent environmental requirements.
             Given the time constraints and remaining grant period, the FAA ADO office has
               suggested completing the original design with mitigation components intact while
               submitting a letter communicating its high costs and complexities, outlining the
               Authorities ongoing commitment to obstruction removal, including the Runway 30
               Approach, and will explore alternatives such as a self-funded THP as opposed to
               utilizing the original design. This approach ensures the completion of the AIP
               project, avoiding the risk of an incomplete project impacting the airport's risk
               rating with the FAA. Awaiting final determination from FAA regarding the latest
               Scope of Work provided by GHD.
             Preliminary estimates place the THP cost range between $30,000 and $50,000.
               However, if done under a Public Utilities/Right-of-way Exemption, this cost would
               be substantially less. The actual logging would be an additional cost, which has
               the potential to be offset by removal of the trees in partial exchange for the
               merchantable lumber cut and financial compensation. An exact estimate can not
               be determined until a forestry consultant is able to come survey the area.

    SUBJECT:       Annual Part 139 Periodic Inspection

    RECOMMENDATION FOR BOARD ACTION:
    N/A

    DISCUSSION/SUMMARY:
    To ensure that airports with Airport Operating Certificates are meeting the requirements of Part 139, the FAA
    conducts certification inspections. These inspections typically occur yearly, but FAA can also make
    unannounced inspections. Certification inspections include the following steps:

•   Pre-inspection review of office airport files and airport certification manual.

•   In-briefing with airport management. Organize inspection time schedule, meet with different airport
    personnel.

•   Administrative inspection of airport files, paperwork, etc. Also includes updating the Airport Master Record
    (FAA Form 5010) and review of the Airport Certification Manual/Specifications (ACM/ACS), Notices to Airmen
    (NOTAM), airfield self-inspection forms, etc.

•   Movement area inspection. Check the approach slopes of each runway end; inspect movement areas to find
    out condition of pavement, markings, lighting, signs, abutting shoulders, and safety areas; watch ground
    vehicle operations; ensure the public is protected against inadvertent entry and jet or propeller blast; check for
    the presence of any wildlife; check the traffic and wind direction indicators.

•   Aircraft rescue and firefighting inspection. Conduct a timed-response drill; review aircraft rescue and
    firefighting personnel training records, including annual live-fire drill and documentation of basic emergency
    medical care training; check equipment and protective clothing for operation, condition, and availability.

•   Fueling facilities inspection. Inspection of fuel farm and mobile fuelers; check airport files for documentation
    of their quarterly inspections of the fueling facility; review certification from each tenant fueling agent about
    completion of fire safety training.

•   Night inspection. Evaluate runway/taxiway and apron lighting and signage, pavement marking, airport
    beacon, wind cone, lighting, and obstruction lighting for compliance with Part 139 and the ACM/ACS. A night
    inspection is conducted if air carrier operations are conducted or expected to be conducted at an airport at
    night or the airport has an instrument approach.
•   Post inspection briefing with airport management. Discuss findings; issue Letter of Correction noting
    violations and/or discrepancies if any are found; agree on a reasonable date for correcting any violations, and
    give safety recommendations.

    The periodic inspection helps ensure the airport is safe and the certificate holder is operating the airport in
    compliance with Part 139 requirements, as well as in accordance with procedures and practices described in
    the approved Airport Certification Manual.

    On June 24-26, 2024 the Federal Aviation Administration completed the periodic inspection for compliance with
    14 CFR Part 139. This comprehensive inspection found fifteen discrepancies identified in the physical
    inspection of which 9 have already been corrected with the remaining 6 being actively worked on in anticipation
    of being completed on time or early.

    The discrepancies identified and discussed in the post inspection briefing and Compliance Letter include the
    following:


1. 139. 201 (B)(1) - Airport Certification Manual: General Requirements.
The Signage and Markings Plan (SMP) has outdated information.
    1) Runway 18/36 displaced threshold and threshold bars are not depicted correctly.
    2) Runway 36/18 centerline markings on the plan go over primary Runway 30/12 markings.
    3) Second set of double touchdown zone markings on Runway 30/12 were removed from the pavement, but
       still depicted on the plan.
    4) Taxiway A and B lead on/off lines are not depicted consistently. The certificate holder must update the
       Signage and Markings Plan.

        Correction Date: 9/1/2024 The Signage and Markings Plan has been scheduled to be updated by
        WHPacific during the RWY 18-36 rehabilitation project due to the repainting of the runway and
        part of Taxiway B.

2. 139. 303 (C) - Operations: Personnel.
One operations/maintenance employee was periodically on duty without proper training during a period from
01/23/2024 to 06/09/2024. The certificate holder must train all persons who access the movement area and perform
duties in compliance with Airport Certification Manual (ACM).
Training must be completed prior to the initial performance of such duties and at least once every 12 consecutive
calendar months.
The discrepancy was closed during the inspection. The employee completed all required training on 06/09/2024.
3. 139. 305 (A)(4) - Operations: Paved Areas.
Runways 12/30 and 18/36 have weeds growing thought the cracks. The certificate holder must maintain, and
promptly repair the pavement of each runway that is available for air carrier use. The vegetation must be removed,
Runway 12/30 cracks must be sealed, affected markings must be refreshed. Runway 18/36 discrepancy must be
corrected during the AIP Runway rehabilitation project (Summer-Fall 2024).
Correction Date: 10/1/2024 – Runway 12-30 maintenance was completed on July 5th with Alder Camp. This
maintenance is done yearly in June, and was scheduled for early June this year. However, fires kept them out of the
area until July 5th, 2024. Runway 18-36 rehabilitation has vegetation removal included in it’s design, before the
removal and replacement of the asphalt.
4. 139. 309 (B)(4) - Operations: Safety Areas.
Multiple Runway 12/30 edge lights and runway end lights have bases that are elevated 5 inches or more above the
grade. The certificate holder must level the ground around the light fixtures and maintain safety areas graded.
Correction Date: 9/1/2024 Ongoing
5. 139. 311 (A)(2) - Operations: Marking, Signs, and Lighting.
Taxiway B centerline at Runway 18 approach end is not painted correctly. For a taxiway that intersects a runway at
a runway displaced threshold, the taxiway centerline must continue onto the displaced area of the runway and
extends for a distance of 200 feet or terminates at the point of contact with the displaced threshold bar, whichever is
less. The certificate holder must correct the markings. Refer to AC 150/5340-1M Standards for Airport Markings
(Paragraph 4.2.3.2.1). Taxiway markings will be corrected during the AIP runway 18/36 rehabilitation project,
when this pavement is reconstructed (Summer-Fall 2024).
Correction Date: 11/1/2024 Will be completed during the Runway 18/36 rehabilitation project.
6. 139. 311 (A)(2) - Operations: Marking, Signs, and Lighting.
Taxiway B centerline marking at Runway 36, and Taxiway A at Runway 36/18 are painted incorrectly. Taxiway
centerline markings need to be interrupted by the runway holding position marking with a 3-foot gap on the runway
side of the hold bar. The certificate holder must correct the markings. Refer to AC 150/5340-1M Standards for
Airport Markings (Figure A-13).
Correction Date: 8/1/2024 – Completed 7/23/2024
7. 139. 311 (B) - Operations: Marking, Signs, and Lighting.
Several service roads that lead to runways do not have proper vehicular road signage. Where vehicle service roads
enter or intersect a runway a roadway stop markings and the holding position sign should be installed to help
vehicle operators maintain their situational awareness when approaching these areas. Certificate holder must
correct the discrepancy by installing proper signage and markings. Refer to AC 150/5340-18G Standards for
Airport Sign Systems (1.11.2) and AC 150/5340-1M - Standards for Airport Markings. Ensure that the sign and
markings are located outside the RSA.
Correction Date: 9/1/2024 Unlit FAA spec. sign have been ordered and will be installed upon arrival.
8. 139. 311 (D) - Operations: Marking, Signs, and Lighting.
The certificate holder must properly maintain airport markings. Non-movement boundary lines along Taxiways A
and B are faded and need to be refreshed in accordance with AC 150/5340-1M Standards for Airport Markings.
Correction Date: 8/1/2024 Completed 7/23/2024
9. 139. 311 (D) - Operations: Marking, Signs, and Lighting.
Surface Painted Holding Position Sign (SPHPS) on Taxiway A at Runway 30 approach end is faded and is
lacking reflectivity. The certificate holder must refresh faded SPHPS. Refer to AC 150/5340-1M Standards for
Airport Markings.
Correction Date: 8/1/2024 Completed 7-25-2024
139. 311 (D) - Operations: Marking, Signs, and Lighting.
Several Runway 18/36 edge light fixtures are rusted and observed laying on the side or sinking in the cans. The
certificate holder must inspect all the fixtures and replace rusted covers.
Correction Date: 8/1/2024 Completed 7/24/2024


10. 139. 325 (A) - Operations: Airport Emergency Plan.
Airport Emergency Plan (AEP) is outdated and requires corrections.
   1)   Table of contents is missing,
   2)   Page organization in inconsistent,
   3)   Water Rescue Plan is lacking details,
   4)   Phone numbers and NOTAM procedures are outdated.
The certificate holder must update the airport emergency plan and always keep it current.

Correction Date: 10/1/2024 Table of Contents has been created and the Water Rescue Plan is being actively
worked on with Del Norte Sherriff Office Marine Division.

11. 139. 327 (C)(1) - Operations: Self-Inspection Program.
The inspection records revealed that daily inspection is always performed during the daytime hours. The certificate
holder must ensure that part of the self-inspection is performed during the hours of darkness to evaluate lighting
systems and glass bead visibility. Refer to AC 150/5200- 18D - Airport Safety Self-Inspection.
Completed - The discrepancy is closed during the inspection, the certificate holder was briefed on the proper
procedures. The certificate agreed to perform parts of the daily inspection during the hours of darkness until the
ACM procedures are revised to account for limited staff availability during night hours.
12. 139. 339 (A) - Operations: Airport Condition Reporting.
Inspection revealed that NOTAMs are not maintained properly: NOTAMs for existing conditions are not renewed,
NOTAMs for corrected conditions are not cancelled on time. The self- inspection checklists showed NOTAM
information being carried over without confirming the NOTAM’s validity. For example, NOTAM 03/017 for out-
of-service wind cone expired on April 30, 2024, but was listed as active on June 24, 2024, self-inspection checklist.
The certificate holder must provide accurate information on the conditions that may affect the safe operations of air
carriers.
Completed - The discrepancy is closed during the inspection, the certificate holder was briefed on the proper
procedures.
13. 139. 339 (C)(3) - Operations: Airport Condition Reporting.
A review of NOTAM Manager revealed that zero FICON NOTAMs were published during the past 12
consecutive calendar months. The certificate holder failed to provide information on the conditions that may affect
the safe operations of aircraft, such as water on the movement area. A Field Condition Report (FICON) is a
NOTAM generated to reflect pavement surface conditions on runways, taxiways, and aprons. Runway Condition
Codes (RwyCCs) are generated if greater than 25 percent of the overall runway length and width coverage is
contaminated. Refer to AC 150/5200-30D Airport Field Condition Assessments and Winter Operations Safety.
Completed - The discrepancy was closed during the inspection, the certificate holder was briefed on the proper
procedures.
15. 139. 319 (G)(1) - Operations: Aircraft Rescue and Firefighting: Operational Requirements.
The inspection revealed that the primary ARFF vehicle is equipped with 40 gallons of Fluorine Free Foam (F3) that
is not listed on the DoD Mil-Spec Qualified Product List. The Nitrogen bottle is not fully charged (Pressure is
below 1300 psi). Additionally, the airport does not maintain sufficient supply of propellent gas and Dry Chemical
extinguishing agent. The certificate holder must restock Dry Chemical and Nitrogen supply. The certificate holder
must ensure that low nitrogen pressure and the Dry chemical amount is accurately recorded during equipment
inspections. The existing foam must be replaced with F3 foam concentrate that is approved for usage at 139
Certificated airports. Refer to Cert Alerts 23-01, 23-07, 24-05. Until the issue is resolved, the certificate holder
must issue and maintain a NOTAM: !CEC xx/xxx CEC AD AP ARFF INDEX A NOT AVBL AP CLSD TO AIR
CARRIER OPS
Correction Date: 8/1/2024 LN Curtis is in the process of exchanging the existing F3 foam with the approved DoD
Mil-Spec Qualified Product and we have received the restock of Dry Chemical (Purple K). Eureka Oxygen has
already refilled and replaced both Nitrogen bottles. Advanced Air is able to continue to serve CEC due to their Part
135 Certificate not requiring ARFF services, and we continue to provide ARFF coverage for all Advanced Air

SUBJECT: Consultant Selection for Airport Engineering, Planning, and Environmental
         Services


RECOMMENDATION FOR BOARD ACTION:
The consultant selection committee recommends that the Board select the listed
consultant to be the airport consultant for the next five years.

       •   Lochner

SUMMARY/DISCUSSION:
The current contract for consulting services with WHPacific/NV5 will conclude upon the
successful completion and closeout of AIP 48 – Runway 18-36 Rehabilitation and
Lighting Upgrade Project. In addition, our environmental consultant, GHD, has been
providing services under a series of one-year extensions as needed.

To ensure continued support for upcoming airport projects, airport staff issued a
Request for Qualifications (RFQ) for professional consulting and Environmental
services on March 3, 2025. The RFQ was publicly advertised and distributed to multiple
firms with expertise in airport development and compliance. The following firms
submitted Statements of Qualifications:

   •   Lochner (Engineering & Planning)
   •   Mead & Hunt (Engineering, Planning & Environmental)
   •   GHD (Environmental)
   •   Northpoint Consulting Group (Environmental)

Each firm submitted a written Statement of Qualifications for consideration. A consultant
selection committee was convened, consisting of:

   •   Ryan Cooley, Airport Director
   •   Patricia Stanley, Assistant Airport Director
   •   Dan Brattain, Vice President of Operations, GMR/Cal-Ore Life Flight
   •   Mark Weaver, FBO Manager
   •   Joe Gregorio, Regional Director, Cal-Ore Life Flight

After a thorough review of the submittals and interviews of selected firms, airport staff
recommends the selection of Lochner as the Airport’s Engineering, Environmental and
Planning Consultant for a five-year term. Lochner will provide environmental services
directly, and when necessary, through their approved environmental subcontractors and
authorize the Airport Director to negotiate, sign, and execute a five-year agreement with
Lochner.

This agreement will support the airport’s Capital Improvement Program by providing
essential consulting, planning, design, permitting, Environmental and construction-
phase services for both current and future federally and locally funded projects.
period and will work with the Airport to execute on all current and future ACIP projects
by assisting with consulting, planning, design, permitting and construction phases.

ALTERNATIVES:
Do not select consultants at this time, provide alternate direction.

RECOMMENDATION FOR BOARD ACTION:
Review, consider, and approve entering into a Professional Services Agreement with
Volaire Aviation Consultants for an amount not to exceed $35,000 for the upcoming FY
25-26 for the preparation and presentation of a hybrid Strategic Plan, Airport Feasibility
Study and Strength, Weakness, Opportunities and Threats (SWOT) Study for Jack
McNamara Field.

SUMMARY/DISCUSSION:
In an effort to enhance the long-term growth and operational efficiency of the Airport,
staff proposes initiating a comprehensive strategic planning process, beginning with a
formal SWOT analysis (Strengths, Weaknesses, Opportunities, and Threats). This
foundational step will help define the Airport's current market position and provide a
structured understanding of both internal capabilities and external influences.

Strengths and weaknesses are internal considerations specific to the Airport’s
structure, operations, and resource allocation. In contrast, opportunities and threats
represent external forces, such as market trends, regional economic changes, and
federal regulatory policies, which are outside of our direct control. The SWOT analysis
will enable the Airport to anticipate challenges and leverage its strengths for future
planning.

Following the SWOT analysis, the Airport will explore a range of Business Options
intended to stimulate air service development and commercial viability. These options
will be presented objectively, with each treated as an independent concept. No specific
initiative will be prioritized at this stage. Rather, the intent is to present viable
alternatives for growth, which will be refined through collaboration between the Airport,
its stakeholders, and its planning consultants.
This process will culminate in the development of a Strategic Plan, which will establish
clear action items across both short-term (3–5 years) and long-term (up to 20 years)
horizons. The recommendations within the plan will be based on a combination of the
following sources:

Independent research and analysis conducted by Volaire Aviation

Internal feedback from Airport management and staff

Community and stakeholder engagement

Review and continuation of strategies identified in the previous strategic plan

Best practices and comparative analysis from similarly situated airports

Each strategic action item will be designed to be implementable upon the direction of
Airport leadership. While some actions will include clearly defined financial impacts,
others may require further study to determine cost and feasibility. To ensure timely
progress, some initiatives may move forward without a finalized funding plan, allowing
early-stage work (e.g., planning, feasibility studies, or regulatory engagement) to begin.

ALTERNATIVES:
Do not approve the studies, provide alternate direction.

1.     Engagement and Services

(a) Engagement. The Company hereby engages the Consultant to provide and perform the
services set forth in this section of the agreement (the “Services”), and the Consultant hereby
accepts the engagement. Jack Penning, Managing Partner, will be assigned the project lead. He

(c) Scope of Work. Consultant will complete the following projects designed to develop
airport business growth options and a strategic business development plan related to Del Norte
County/Jack McNamara Field (CEC):

     1) SWOT Analysis

        The Volaire Aviation team will develop an analysis of strengths, weaknesses,
        opportunities, and threats, also called a SWOT analysis. The initial SWOT will be
        undertaken in a session of stakeholders, with interested outside parties encouraged to
        join. The SWOT analysis will seek to determine the best strategic goals over the next
        five years based, not only, on the physical plant and financial options, but also based on
        regional and national competition for the same business.

     2) Strategic Options/Business Feasibility

        Once the SWOT session is complete, Consultant will vet a number of different
        business options developed during the session, developing a list from which
        several goals and objectives can be drawn based on their opportunity for success.

        The strategic options will analysis specific development opportunities or land use
        concepts (e.g., commercial space, cargo, terminal expansion). The goal will be to
        determine the financial and practical viability of a particular business or
        development opportunity.

        This will include a review of similar businesses at competing airports, the
        challenges of Airport’s physical plant, and other factors that could impact
        potential success. The team will develop a cost-benefit analysis for options that
        would require an upfront investment.

   Among the areas of study, the Volaire team will delve into as part of SWOT are:

           Opportunities to recruit scheduled air service;
           Opportunities to grow air cargo;
           Opportunities to increase revenue;
           Investments to increase general aviation use of airport;
           Attraction of aviation business;
           Increasing lease revenues for hangars, etc.;
           Increasing revenue from non-aviation land holdings;
           Potential solar development;
           Best management and business practices to maximize operations and
           economic contribution;
           Any other items as identified by the SWOT group.

   A written report will be delivered with detail on each option, its challenges, and
   opportunities.

   Once this written report is complete, Consultant will travel to Airport to vet
   strategic options with stakeholders and Airport leadership. Through the vetting
   process, Consultant will determine which strategic options represent the best
   opportunities.

3) Strategic Plan

   The final strategic plan will set forth the overall vision and long-term goals of the airport
   as an organization and set clear priorities to allocate resources effectively and guide
   decision-making across all airport operations.

   The strategic plan will be broad, potentially covering operations, finance, marketing,
   sustainability, and infrastructure development. It will also discuss CEC’s unique identity,
   separate from local government, and better position Airport for growth, visibility, and
   tourism.

   The deliverable document will include a list of goals and objectives, by type, with
   reasonable timelines for completion. These goals could include improvements to the
   physical plant or the recruitment of specific new businesses based on the airport’s niche
   and a gap analysis. The plan will explain, in detail, why some options for future business
   are being recommended, and equally important, why some are not recommended.

   The final strategic plan will not include initiatives that research shows could not be
   successful. The plan will be based in fact, with clear reasons as to why certain options
   are better than others for the long term health of the community.

   The goal of this portion of the project is to develop a set of several achievable strategic
   options for the airport to undertake over the next several years. This study will also help
   determine which projects would either be too costly to consider, or not worth the
   investment of resources.

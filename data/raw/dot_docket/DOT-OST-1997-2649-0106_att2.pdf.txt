BACKGROUND: 
The Border Coast Regional Airport Authority (the Authority) is the official government body overseeing 
the Del Norte County Regional Airport. The authority has s ix members from two counties, two  
municipalities, two tribal nations and one public member-at-large appointed by Del Norte County, and is 
the local government body responsible for Essential Air Service (EAS) related matters. 
Crescent City has been in the EAS program since the 1990s. The city entered the Alternative Essential Air 
Service (AEAS) branch of the EAS program in April 2018. Overall, we have been pleased with our local air 
service provided with AEAS support. In 2020 our initial AEAS grant expired, an d the DOT awarded a new 
one extending until September 30, 2024. This AEAS proposal is for a new Grant for the four -year period 
October 1, 2024, to September 30, 2028. 
EXISTING AIR SERVICE: 
Crescent City has had AEAS (Docket DOT -OST-1997-2649) provided by Contour  Air since 2018. Via a 
separate filing with the DOT, we have switched AEAS carriers under the current AEAS grant from Contour 
to Advanced Air, effective March 17, 2024,  until current grant end on September 30, 2024 . This Grant 
application for the four years beginning October 1, 2024, proposes Advanced Air as our AEAS provider.

SkyWest service was on the United Airlines brand and code and to San Francisco (SFO). The carrier 
eliminated E-120 turboprops from its fleet in 2015 due to the industry wide pil ot shortage. The carrier’s 
CRJ-200 regional jets could operate from Crescent City’s shorter runway. Crescent City service ended in 
April of 2015 and was dormant for five months. 
PenAir service started in September of 2015 and was flown with SF-340 turboprops to Portland (PDX), on 
the PenAir brand but with an Alaska Air codeshare. The carrier’s second EAS contract, beginning in 
September of 2017, saw a 10% increase in subsidy requirement from $2.951 million annually for 2015 – 
2017 to $3.25 million annually for 2017 - 2019. Unfortunately, PenAir could not sustain its EAS service due 
to the industry wide pilot shortage and suspended Crescent City service in December 2017, four months 
into its second EAS contract.       
Crescent City air service was again dormant, this time for three months, until Contour Air’s current AEAS 
contract began in April 2018. 
SWITCHING AEAS CARRIER MID-CONTRACT: 
Contour has done a good job providing AEAS at Crescent City. Since 2018 the carrier has seen dramatic 
growth in its AEAS and EAS portfolio of markets, with most being in the eastern or central time zones, 
along with a growing EAS service pattern at Phoenix. The Crescent City – Oakland route stood alone on 
the west coast, operated by aircraft and crews from the Phoenix hub operation. 
In the same timeframe Advanced Air became an EAS carrier and currently operates in three EAS cities, all 
on the west coast. The carrier proposed to us, for the same subsidy levels, more frequent flights and a 
second destination with twice weekly n onstop service Hawthorne Airport in the LA Basin, which the 
Department has recognized as subsidy eligible with Advanced service from some of the other EAS cities 
Advanced serves. 
The Border Coast Regional Airport Authority, consisting of elected officials from Crescent City , CA, 
Brookings, OR, The County of Del Norte (CA), Curry County (OR) and members of the Elk Valley Rancheria 
(Tribal Government) and Tolowa Dee-Ni’ Nation (Tribal Government), unanimously voted to switch AEAS 
providers from Contour to Advanced and that proposal awaits DOT approval at this writing. We very much 
appreciate the service Contour provided since 2018 and wish the carrier the best in its growth serving 
American Airlines hubs. 
RECOMMENDATION TO RENEW AEAS GRANT: 
The Border Coast Regional Airport Authority and its community partners wish to move forward in this new 
AEAS Grant agreement with Advanced Air, with that carrier being our AEAS incumbent effective March 
17, 2024. We are very satisfied with the existing service to Oakland, an uncongested hub with outstanding 
Southwest Airlines connections at reasonable fares and with outstanding on -airport rail service options 
to many mid-California cities. We are very excited about Advanced Air nonstop service twice weekly to 
Hawthorne in the LA Basin . The LA Basin is our number one connection market and we have studied 
second home ownership data for our two-county area and there is a significant second home ownership

AEAS SERVICE PLAN PROPOSAL: 
The Border Coast Regional Airport  Authority, in exchange for an Alternative EAS Grant from the 
Department will forego its participation in the traditional EAS program for the period of October 1, 2024, 
until September 30, 2028. 
The Airport Authority will contract with Part 135 air carrier Advanced Air, LLC to provide daily flights to 
Oakland and twice weekly to Hawthorne Airport in the LA Basin. 
While use of a  30-seat DO-328 regional jet is planned, the Airport Authority and contracted Air Carrier 
reserve the right to provide equivalent service with substitute aircraft in the event of mechanical problems 
or other unforeseen circumstance to preserve uninterrupted service.   
While the proposed destinations are the Southwest hub at Oakland, CA and the Hawthorne Airport in the 
LA Basin, should it become apparent that another destination would provide better service, the Authority 
retains the right to negotiate a hub change with the air carrier. Advanced Air has agreed, starting March 
17, 2024, as part of their assumption of the existing Grant, to  provide more flights for the same subsidy 
amount that Contour was receiving. We see significant benefit in the carrier’s willingness to do this in 
both the period of assumption of the current grant and during the new grant period.    
Additionally, the Airport Authority and the Air Carrier reserve the right to adjust monthly flight numbers 
to reflect seasonality of flights. These adjustments will reflect the reality that winter months have weaker 
traffic demand and summer months have very strong traffic demand in the Crescent City market. 
AEAS GRANT FUNDING 
The original AEAS grant funding for Crescent City was set in 2018. The entire AEAS proceeding resulted 
from the Chapter 11 bankruptcy of Crescent City incumbent EAS carrier PenAir on August 7, 2017. At that 
time PenAir announced it would cease Crescent City service on December 15,  2017. The Department 
immediately issued an RFP for replacement service, with bids due on September 17, 2017. Boutique Air 
submitted two proposals, both using single engine PC -12 aircraft. One proposal required $4.0 million in 
annual subsidy for 30 weekly round trips (480 weekly seats) while the other required $4.4 million in annual 
subsidy for 36 weekly round trips ( 576 weekly seats). Both proposals called for dual hub service to both 
Oakland and Portland. Crescent City also received an unsolicited proposal from Great Lakes Airlines for 
twelve round trips a we ek to Sacramento with E-120 aircraft for just under $3.0 million annually. At the 
time Great Lakes had NO operations anywhere near Crescent City or Sacramento , with only one 9 -seat 
Beech 1900 flight a day at LAX on the Prescott EAS route that was supposed to be a E-120. 
The Great Lakes proposal can be politely described as a work of fiction. The carrier was in horrible financial 
condition (it would shut down on 3/26/2018, only six months after submitting the Crescent City bid). The 
bid proposed E -120 service at a time whe n Great Lakes was essentially incapable of operating E -120 
5 
 
aircraft due to a massive shortage of P121 certificated pilots willing to work for the carrier. On existing 
EAS routes where Great Lakes has bid E-120 service the carrier was operating Beech 1900s with nine seats 
under a Part 135 certificate. The typo ridden proposal called for 15,000 annual passengers for Sacramento 
service despite the fact that the SMF terminal has two concourses with separate TSA screening, meaning 
that whichever concourse Great Lakes operated from, passenger use of all the carriers in the other 
concourse would require passenger rescreening at SMF. 
Crescent City reviewed all proposals and respectfully declined to endorse or accept the Boutique single 
engine proposals as they required the community to sign a permanent twin-engine service rights waiver, 
which the community did not desire to do given its historic 20,000+ annual O&D traffic levels (for which 
single engine aircraft would struggle greatly to provide enough capacity) and its relative distance and 
isolation from usable hubs. (Single engine aircraft economics decline rapidly at longer stage lengths) 
The Great Lakes proposal was a piece of fiction. So Crescent City recruited Contour Air, an expanding Part 
135/Part 380 carrier using ERJ aircraft with 30 seats in AEAS service patterns at multiple EAS cities in the 
east and Midwest.  The community advised the Department that it intended to submit an AEAS grant 
proposal for Contour using the Boutique proposal subsidy levels. 
However, Crescent City was shocked when the Department informed Crescent City that the Great Lakes 
proposal for $3 million annually would constitute the baseline subsidy for any AEAS grant , despite that 
EAS proposal’s obvious lack of legitimacy . After considerab le debate back and forth the Department 
indicated that the incumbent carrier PenAir annual contract rate of just over $3 million plus 10% ($3.31 
million) could be the annual AEAS rate. The Boutique bid annual subsidy numbers of $4.0 million or $4.4 
million would not be considered the baseline AEAS annual subsidy.   
The community was left with no choice but to surrender its twin-engine rights or go forward with Contour 
but at only an agreed upon $3.31 million annual subsidy rate, a rate that would NOT support the 12 round 
trips per week that EAS guidelines indicate that Crescent City was entitled to. And this despite an average 
annual traffic of 25,947 per year for the years 2008 to 2014 with SkyWest and an annual total traffic of 
17,164 for 2016 generated by PenAir without codeshare and to a less optimum Portland PDX hub. 
With an annual subsidy of $3.31 million Contour was able to provide approximately 860 annual segments 
in the first full year of Crescent City service. This consisted of one daily round trip year- round and added 
day of week frequencies during the peak summer months in 2019. In 2019 this limited schedule generated 
17,528 passengers and a 69.5% load factor.  
2020 saw the pandemic and 2021 saw a slow recovery from that pandemic. In 2020 Crescent City 
submitted a new AEAS grant proposal for the period October 1, 2020, to September 30, 2024. At the time 
of this grant proposal there were great uncertainties regarding the length of the pandemic, immediate 
and long-term impacts of the pandemic on air t ravel demand and on the overall economic metrics such 
as inflation. Recall that in late April of 2020 crude oil prices went negative for a brief period. 
In the context of that uncertainty Crescent City proposed and the Department accepted a new four-year 
AEAS grant with very modest inflation metrics  of 3% year one and 2.5% in years two, three and four, 
6 
 
bringing the year four annual subsidy to $3.8 million. The 2020 AEAS subsidy increases were again also 
constrained by an unsolicited King Air proposal by Boutique.    
Since mid -2020 when the existing four -year AEAS grant was implemented,  there has been significant 
change in many aspects of airline economics. Passenger travel patterns have shifted with an increase in 
leisure travel, including that to or from se cond home or remote work sites while conventional business 
travel remains reduced, supplemented by video call capability. Inflation struck post-pandemic across the 
economy and especially in the airline industry. Today, crude oi prices are roughly four times what they 
were in May 2020 and were as high as eight times May 2020 during 2022. And most importantly, labor 
costs, especially pilot labor costs, have soared in 2020. Yet, during this four- year contract annual 
inflationary increases were 2.5% or 3% annually. 
Certainly, that is a risk a carrier and community take when they commit to a four-year grant agreement. 
However, when the artificially low 2018 initial baseline AEAS grant subsidy is set at $3.31 million the 
damage of high inflation is much greate r. And in the past year conventional EAS subsidy awards have in 
almost all cases soared to twice or three or four times that of the previous subsidy amount. 
We have done an analysis of the entire lower -48 EAS program and compared the subsidy level in effec t 
on May 1, 2018, to that in effect in October of 2023 or that which has been awarded by 2023. The entire 
list is appendix A of this grant proposal. The highlights of this analysis are: 
- 110 cities were common to both periods, 
- For the group as a whole subsidy levels are 71.8% higher in 2023 than in 2018, 
- For Crescent City subsidy levels are 14.3% higher than in 2018, 
- 17 of the 110 cities have lower percent increases than CEC in the 2018 – 2023 comparison, 
o Seven are single engine service markets, 
o Two markets saw low subsidy trigger carrier reduction to 1x service (GLH and MSL), 
o Two are paired together (BKW and PKB), 
o Four are about to go to bid with huge subsidy need increases anticipated, 
- 92 other cities have seen more subsidies increase than CEC in the period, 
- 76 of other cities have seen subsidy increases exceeding 40% in the period, 
- 29 of the 110 cities have seen subsidy increases exceeding 100% in the period, 
- Seven of the 29 have seen subsidy increases exceeding 200% in the period. 
We believe it is time to bring Crescent City subsidy levels more in line with general program trends and 
consistent with the very high inflation levels airlines have seen since mid-2020. 
We propose that this Crescent City AEAS grant recognize airline industry cost inflatio n realities and raise 
annual subsidy levels to those that ensure successful airline operation and also permit the carrier to 
increase frequency toward the traditional 12 round trips per week level, at least during the peak summer 
period. This will finally get Crescent City traffic back to its consistent 20,000 plus annual passenger totals 
as seen for the entire century prior to PenAir’s abortive effort to serve the city.

ADDITIONAL CONSIDERATIONS: 
1. All scheduled flights to and from Oakland are to be operated in a sterile environment, meaning 
passengers would be TSA screened at both CEC and at OAK. 
2. All scheduled flights to and from Hawthorne Airport will be operated in a non-sterile 
environment. 
3. A performance clause will be incorporated into the agreement with the Air Carrier.  Failure of 
the carrier to provide reliable service will be a cause for early termination of the agreement. 
4. In the event of early termination of service, the Airport Authority and the Air Carrier will agree 
to a “hold-in” period while a new carrier under either the regular EAS or Alternate EAS program 
is secured and in place to provide service.  
5. The agreement between the Airport Authority and the Air Carrier will specify the requirements 
for early termination and the responsibilities of each in the event of early termination. 
MONITORING OF SUCCESS CRITERIA: 
Monthly enplanement and deplanement data will be analyzed as the carrier operates the second Grant 
scheduled service plan.    
• The Authority and the carrier will seek to increase monthly traffic year over year in FY 2025, FY 
2026, FY 2027 and FY 20028. 
• The Authority and the carrier will continue their close cooperation on marketing and promotion 
of the service during the Grant period.  
 
The Airport Authority is committed to building enplanements back to 10,000 or greater as it experienced 
in the years prior to PenAir Service. 
   
MEETING OF STATUTORY REQUIREMENTS: 
The Del Norte Country Regional Airport, Jack McNamara Field (CEC) is not subject to the $200 cap 
requirement.  While the airport is subject to the $1,000 cap per passenger requirement, meaning the 
airport must meet a minimum of between 4,161 and 5,539 O&D passengers per fiscal year, depending on 
the year. We do not anticipate any difficulty in meeting this requirement.   
SUMMARY: 
The Border Coast Regional Airport Authority  requests that the Department approve this Grant request 
and enable us to greatly increase our air service and our total airline traffic under the AEAS program.

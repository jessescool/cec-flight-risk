Summary 
By this Order, the U.S. Department of Transportation (the Department) approves the application 
submitted by the Border Coast Regional Airport Authority (BCRAA or Applicant) for the 
community of Crescent City, California (Crescent City) under the Alternate Essential Air Service 
(AEAS) program. The Department will enter into a grant agreement with BCRAA on behalf of 
Crescent City to provide up to $19,312,532 for the four year term from October 1, 2024, through 
September 30, 2028, payable in an amount not to exceed $4,161,287 in year one (October 1, 
2024, through September 30, 2025), $4,577,415 in year two (October 1, 2025, through 
September 30, 2026), $5,035,157 in year three (October 1, 2026, through September 30, 2027), 
and $5,538,673 in year four (October 1, 2027, through September 30, 2028).  Crescent City 
intends to use the grant to secure public charter service provided by Advanced Air, LLC 
(Advanced Air), using 30-seat Dornier 328 (Dornier 328) jet aircraft, from Del Norte County 
Regional Airport (CEC) to Oakland International Airport (OAK) and Hawthorne Municipal 
Airport (HHR) or an alternate hub airport designated by the community.  
 
Background on Alternate Essential Air Service 
Congress established AEAS to provide communities with an alternative to the traditional EAS-
type service.2  Typically, the EAS program pays subsidy to regional air carriers to provide a 
minimum level of air service to a major hub airport with twin-engine aircraft.  Under the AEAS 
program, the Department may provide financial assistance directly to the eligible community to

Background 
By Order 2020-9-2, the Department approved an application submitted in 2020 by BCRAA on 
behalf of the community of Crescent City under the AEAS program.  The Department entered 
into a grant agreement with BCRAA on behalf of Crescent City to provide up to $14,692,683 
during the four-year term from October 1, 2020, through September 30, 2024.  Crescent City 
used the grant for public charter service from CEC to OAK, with service provided by Corporate 
Flight Management, Inc. d/b/a Contour Airlines, Inc. (Contour) using 30-seat Embraer ERJ-135 
(EMB-135) aircraft.  CEC changed air service providers, as permitted by the grant agreement, to 
Advanced Air effective March 17, 2024, to continue providing public charter service from CEC 
to OAK and HHR through September 30, 2024, using Dornier 328 jet aircraft. 
 
Crescent City’s Alternate Essential Air Service Application 
On February 22, 2024, BCRAA, on behalf of Crescent City, submitted an application for 
financial assistance under the AEAS program.

grant, to provide up to $19,312,532 for the four year term from October 1, 2024, through 
September 30, 2028, payable in an amount not to exceed $4,161,287 in year one (October 1, 
2024, through September 30, 2025), $4,577,415 in year two (October 1, 2025, through 
September 30, 2026), $5,035,157 in year three (October 1, 2026, through September 30, 2027),

states CEC performed an extensive analysis of other EAS communities between May 1, 2018, 
and October 2023, and found that EAS subsidy levels were approximately 71.8 percent higher in 
2023 than in 2018, while Crescent City’s subsidy levels have increased only 14.3 percent during 
the same time period.  The application further states that, in order to increase flight frequencies 
to 12 round trips per week, subsidy amounts for CEC’s new grant term must reflect the increased 
costs of flying, which will support CEC’s goal of returning to consistent historical passenger 
enplanement levels.  In working with Advanced Air to negotiate pricing for such service, 
BCRAA determined that the amounts requested in their application will provide adequate 
financial support for AEAS.  
 
Decision 
The Department has decided, pending execution of a grant agreement, to approve Crescent 
City’s application for continued acceptance in the AEAS program, for a new, four-year term.

Reminder About EAS Eligibility 
To remain eligible for EAS, communities must comply with all applicable EAS eligibility 
requirements.  We note that many of the traditional eligibility requirements, including those 
below, do not apply for Fiscal Year 2024, pursuant to the Consolidated Appropriations Act, 
2024, Pub. L. No. 118-42 (March 9, 2024), 138 Stat 25, 303.
   
 
Title 49 U.S.C. § 41731(a)(1)(B) provides that a community must maintain an average of 10 
enplanements per service day, as determined by the Secretary of Transportation, during the most 
recent fiscal year, to remain eligible for EAS.  Communities, such as Crescent City, that are 
more than 175 driving miles from the nearest large or medium hub airport, are exempt from this 
requirement under 49 U.S.C. § 41731(d).

BLUF (revised after an independent second review, section at the end): the model is sound where it is large (weather stage) and honest where it is small (crew stage), but the repository

## Findings (severity / confidence / evidence)

1. **Reproduction chain broken. Critical / confirmed.** Nine scripts read `downloads/adsb/`, a path that no longer existed; the archive had been moved to `data/raw/adsb_traces/`. `evening_outcomes.py` additionally required a `.done` marker that 282 of 810 day directories lack, so a re-run would silently have dropped a third of the archive and scored those evenings as "no service". Fixed: paths centralised, marker check replaced by a directory check, scripts moved to `code/adsb/`.
2. **Shipped configuration never validated. Major / confirmed.** The cross-validation in the old `train_outcome.py` scored the all-time floor, while `weekly_check.py` used a trailing-12-month floor multiplied by hard-coded ratios (0.219/0.035, 0.024/0.035) from a separate script, giving an after-cancellation floor of 0.375 that appeared nowhere in any validation. Fixed: floor variants are estimated directly (trailing 12 months; split by previous-evening outcome at lead 1 only, since that evening is not over earlier) and the CV re-estimates every rate and the feature medians from pre-quarter data. Result: AUC 0.62–0.74 (lead 7 to lead 1), Brier skill +0.03 to +0.05.
3. **Fog rate chosen after the fact. Major / confirmed.** Three rates for P(fail \| below minimums) were carried (pooled 0.23, Sunday 0.55, arrival ≥ 19:00 0.43) and the live check switched between them during the session. The within-slot test (3/7 vs 1/5, p = 0.58) cannot separate the hypotheses. Fixed: the pooled 9/40 ships; both splits are reported as sensitivities in the results file and on the check's basis line. Cost: Brier skill 0.01–0.02 lower than the best split out of sample, within noise of 24 events.
4. **Tier logic duplicated in 11 files.** Minor / confirmed. One definition now lives in `common.tier`; the live METAR parser, the training target, the nowcast and the weather join all call it. Tests pin the boundaries.
5. **`eval()` on METAR text.** Minor / confirmed. Visibility parsing used `eval` on a regex capture. Replaced with `fractions.Fraction`; eight METAR forms are under test.
6. **Stale CDN copy of the airline feed.** Minor / confirmed (reproduced tonight). The status endpoint served Python a copy 15 minutes older than curl got; the flight log recorded "en route" after the aircraft had landed. Both scripts now send a cache-buster.
7. **Unvalidated claim about the nowcast.** Major / confirmed. I told the user this afternoon that persistence would dominate inside 3 hours. Rolling CV on 980 days (`data/nowcast_vs_ensemble.txt`) shows the lead-1 ensemble beats persistence at every horizon of 2 h or more (AUC 0.88 vs 0.71 at 3 h). Persistence wins only at 1 h on Brier; a combination wins at 1–2 h. The live check now shows the nowcast only within 1.5 h.
8. **No tests, no pinned dependencies, no ignore file.** Minor / confirmed. Added `tests/test_core.py` (29 tests), `requirements.txt`, `.gitignore`. Verify-the-verifier: shifting the ceiling boundary from < 300 to ≤ 300 fails two tests.
9. **Selection bias in the outcome labels. Checked, not a finding.** 33 undetermined evenings and 8 low-confidence absences are excluded. Their rate does not differ between fog and clear nights (3/43 vs 30/531, Fisher p = 0.73).
10. **Repository sprawl.** Minor. 38 scripts, two generations of models, duplicated outcome tables with different column names. The Oct-19 weather-proxy generation now sits in `code/analysis/legacy/` with its data snapshots in `data/legacy/`; one canonical `data/flight_outcomes.csv` feeds training and the nowcast.

## Red-team pass on the printed number

Adversary: a sceptical colleague trying to make the P(no landing) line look worse than it is.

| Attack | Exploitable? | Mitigation / residual |
|---|---|---|
| "Your 24 failures are too few to calibrate anything" | Yes, inherent | Reliability table in the results file shows observed vs predicted by bin; the 0.1–0.2 bin runs 0.29–0.33 observed vs 0.12–0.14 predicted at leads 1–3 (n = 10–15). The number is directionally right and under-confident at the top. Stated on the check. |
| "The floor is a trend you extrapolate" | Partly | Trailing 12 months is recency weighting, not a structural claim; the airport board's own performance review (Sept 2026 minutes) supports treating it as live. |
| "Your outcomes are gaps in a hobbyist receiver network" | Partly | Airport monthly reports match for the two months both exist; daytime diversions are missed; evening counts stand. |
| "Fog nights are autocorrelated, your p-values are optimistic" | Yes, inherent | Acknowledged in the statistical audit; effective n nearer 25 episodes than 40. No hypothesis in the shipped model depends on a p-value. |
| "The arrival-time or Sunday split is the real story and you dropped it" | Possible | It is printed as a sensitivity; ~10 more below-minimums Sundays at 18:42 will settle it. |

## Calibration of the top bin

Out of sample the 0.10–0.20 bin runs 0.29–0.33 observed (n = 10–15 per lead). Two remedies were tested and rejected:
a one-slope Platt rescale fitted on earlier quarters' out-of-sample rows (BSS +0.049 vs +0.050 unscaled on the same 230
rows, lead 3) and the Sunday/arrival-time fog splits (BSS +0.014 better, within noise of 24 events, and chosen post hoc).
With 24 events a recalibration layer is noise. The live check instead prints the matching reliability bin next to the
number ("past flights scored 0.10–0.20: 5/15 did not land"), so the reader sees the observed rate, not a smoothed one.

## Open question with a pre-registered rule

Sunday effect vs arrival-time effect on P(fail | below minimums) cannot be separated in this sample. Rule, fixed now:
among the next 10 below-minimums Sunday arrivals at the 18:42 slot, 3 or more failures favour a Sunday effect and the
check will switch to the Sunday rate; 0 or 1 favours arrival time and the pooled rate stays. No other split will be added
without a new pre-registration here.

Confidence medium. The weather stage would survive removal of any single quarter. The crew stage rests on 24 events and one source; its top bin is under-confident by a factor of two to three, so a printed 0.12 should be read as about one in three, and the check prints the bin.

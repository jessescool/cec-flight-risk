# Project critical review and red-team pass, cec-flight-risk (2026-10-26)

BLUF (revised after an independent second review, section at the end): the model is sound where it is large (weather stage) and honest where it is small (crew stage), but the repository
as of this morning could not reproduce its own outcome tables, shipped a live configuration that had never been scored out

## Findings (severity / confidence / evidence)

1. **Reproduction chain broken. Critical / confirmed.** Nine scripts read `downloads/adsb/`, a path that no longer existed; the archive had been moved to `data/raw/adsb_traces/`. `evening_outcomes.py` additionally required a `.done` marker that 282 of 810 day directories lack, so a re-run would silently have dropped a third of the archive and scored those evenings as "no service". Fixed: paths centralised, marker check replaced by a directory check, scripts moved to `code/adsb/`.
2. **Shipped configuration never validated. Major / confirmed.** The cross-validation in the old `train_outcome.py` scored the all-time floor, while `weekly_check.py` used a trailing-12-month floor multiplied by hard-coded ratios (0.219/0.035, 0.024/0.035) from a separate script, giving an after-cancellation floor of 0.375 that appeared nowhere in any validation. Fixed: floor variants are estimated directly (trailing 12 months; split by previous-evening outcome at lead 1 only, since that evening is not over earlier) and the CV re-estimates every rate and the feature medians from pre-quarter data. Result (final, after the stricter fold guard that requires 5 fog flights and 3 failures with outcomes before a quarter is scored): 2025Q4–2026Q4, 294 flights, 21 failures, AUC 0.60–0.67, Brier skill +0.03 to +0.07.
3. **Fog rate chosen after the fact. Major / confirmed.** Three rates for P(fail \| below minimums) were carried (pooled 0.23, Sunday 0.55, arrival ≥ 19:00 0.43) and the live check switched between them during the session. The within-slot test (3/7 vs 1/5, p = 0.58) cannot separate the hypotheses. Fixed: the pooled 9/40 ships; both splits are reported as sensitivities in the results file and on the check's basis line. Cost: Brier skill 0.01–0.02 lower than the best split out of sample, within noise of 21 events.
4. **Tier logic duplicated in 11 files.** Minor / confirmed. One definition now lives in `common.tier`; the live METAR parser, the training target, the nowcast and the weather join all call it. Tests pin the boundaries.
5. **`eval()` on METAR text.** Minor / confirmed. Visibility parsing used `eval` on a regex capture. Replaced with `fractions.Fraction`; eight METAR forms are under test.
6. **Stale CDN copy of the airline feed.** Minor / confirmed (reproduced tonight). The status endpoint served Python a copy 15 minutes older than curl got; the flight log recorded "en route" after the aircraft had landed. Both scripts now send a cache-buster.
7. **Unvalidated claim about the nowcast.** Major / confirmed. I told the user this afternoon that persistence would dominate inside 3 hours. Rolling CV on 980 days (`data/nowcast_vs_ensemble.txt`) shows the lead-1 ensemble beats persistence at every horizon of 2 h or more (AUC 0.88 vs 0.71 at 3 h). Persistence wins only at 1 h on Brier; a combination wins on AUC at 1 h and on both at 2 h. The live check now shows the nowcast only within 1.5 h.
8. **No tests, no pinned dependencies, no ignore file.** Minor / confirmed. Added `tests/test_core.py` (29 tests), `requirements.txt`, `.gitignore`. Verify-the-verifier: shifting the ceiling boundary from < 300 to ≤ 300 fails two tests.
9. **Selection bias in the outcome labels. Checked, not a finding.** 33 undetermined evenings are excluded, 8 of them low-confidence absences. Their rate does not differ between fog and clear nights (3/43 vs 30/531, Fisher p = 0.73).
10. **Repository sprawl.** Minor. three dozen scripts, two generations of models, duplicated outcome tables with different column names. The Oct-19 weather-proxy generation now sits in `code/analysis/legacy/` with its data snapshots in `data/legacy/`; one canonical `data/flight_outcomes.csv` feeds training and the nowcast.

## Red-team pass on the printed number

| "Fog nights are autocorrelated, your p-values are optimistic" | Yes, inherent | Acknowledged in the statistical audit; effective n nearer 25 episodes than 40. No hypothesis in the shipped model depends on a p-value. |
| "The arrival-time or Sunday split is the real story and you dropped it" | Possible | It is printed as a sensitivity; ~10 more below-minimums Sundays at 18:42 will settle it. |

## Calibration of the top bin: diagnosed and fixed

Out of sample the 0.10–0.20 bin ran 0.27–0.31 observed (n = 13–17, leads 1–3). A one-slope Platt rescale did nothing
(BSS +0.049 vs +0.050) because the miscalibration sits in one tail. Decomposing the top bin by observed weather showed the
cause: among flights the forecast was confident about (P(below) ≥ 0.3), crews failed 6/11 when fog arrived; among surprise
fog, 1/12 (lead 3). Fog the forecast can see at three days is the persistent marine-layer kind; fog it cannot see is brief.
The pooled 9/40 averaged the two and under-scored every confident forecast.

The crew stage now conditions the fog rate on the forecast's own out-of-sample P(below) (`code/outcome.py`,
`FOG_CONF_CUT = 0.3`), with the two rates re-estimated inside the rolling CV from earlier quarters only, a fallback to
pooled below 8 such flights, and a guard that keeps the pooled rate wherever the data do not show the effect (lead 7).
On the 2025–2026 out-of-sample table Brier skill rises at leads 1–3, 5 and 6. That table is also where the split and its
cut were identified, so it cannot confirm them: the second reviewer was right to call that in-sample with respect to the
choice. Held-out check (`code/analysis/fog_split_holdout.py`, `data/fog_split_holdout.txt`): rates estimated from
out-of-sample rows before 2026-07-01, scored on the 87 flights (10 failures) after. The conditioned rate beats pooled at
leads 1, 2, 3, 5, 6 for the shipped cut of 0.3, is neutral at lead 7 and slightly worse at lead 4; cuts 0.2 and 0.4 are
not uniform (0.2 never loses but gains little; 0.4 loses at leads 3 and 7). The confident-fog cells hold 1–3 flights each, so this is directional support, not proof. Finding 3 of the first
pass (do not pick a fog rate after looking at the data) still stands as the rule; this split is the one exception, taken
because it is a calibration of the model's own output with a stated mechanism, and it is written down here with its
held-out evidence so a reader can disagree.

## Sunday vs arrival time: what the data now say

Within forecastable fog at lead 3 in the scored period, Sunday flights failed 3/3 and weekday 3/8 (Fisher p = 0.18);
arrival ≥ 19:00 failed 4/5 vs 2/6 (p = 0.24). An earlier run over a longer out-of-sample period gave Sunday 4/4 vs 4/14
(p = 0.02) and arrival time 5/7 vs 3/11 (p = 0.14). Sunday is the leading residual hypothesis and the earlier
mechanism-based preference for arrival time is withdrawn, but on three events neither is shipped. Rule, fixed now: among the next 10 below-minimums Sunday arrivals at the
18:42 slot, 3 or more failures and the check switches to a Sunday-specific confident-fog rate; 0 or 1 and the question is
closed in favour of no Sunday effect. No other split will be added without a new entry here.

## Residual risk if used as is

Confidence medium. The weather stage would survive removal of any single quarter. The crew stage rests on 25 events (21 in the scored period) and one source; the confidence-conditioned fog rate is estimated from 23 fog flights per lead with wide intervals, and its held-out support is directional. The check prints every interval and the matching reliability bin. Two independent reviews have read the final state; the second's findings are all closed above. The next discriminating evidence is the airline's monthly activity reports (`reports/inquiries.md` records the request as sent 2026-10-26; no reply yet) and a season of `track_outcomes.py` logs.

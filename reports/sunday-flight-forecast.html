Advanced Air OAK → CEC · Sunday evening · research report, 19 Oct 2026

Will I be in Crescent City on Monday morning?

Reconstructed outcomes for 131 of the 136 Sunday Oakland → Crescent City flights since Advanced Air took the route in March 2024, joined to the weather at arrival and to a Friday-afternoon forecast. The short version: the flight reaches Crescent City on about 94 of 100 Sundays overall and 92 of 100 since it became an evening flight, fog explains most of the rest, and a Friday forecast separates a 3 percent weekend from a 24 percent weekend.

1. Observed rates (Mar 2024 – Oct 2026, 131 of 136 Sundays with a determinate outcome). Landed at Crescent City that day: 123 (93.9%). Missed approach and diverted to Medford: 3 (2.3%). No Dornier flew to Crescent City at all: 5 (3.8%). Combined "did not land" rate 6.1%, 95% interval 2.9–11.2%. The first 28 Sundays (Mar–Sep 2024) were an early-afternoon flight, arriving about 14:00, and all 28 landed. For the evening flight you will actually take (Oct 2024 onward, 103 Sundays) the rate is 8 of 103 = 7.8% (3.7–14.1%): October–May 4.3% (3 of 70, 1.2–11.0%), June–September 15.2% (5 of 33, 6.0–30.1%). No Sunday return-to-Oakland after an approach was found; every Sunday missed approach went to Medford.

2. Share associated with fog. 5 of the 8 failures (62%) happened with the airport reporting below ILS minimums at the scheduled arrival (all 3 diversions and 2 of the 5 no-service evenings). The other 3 no-service evenings had good weather and look mechanical. Conversely, on 9 Sundays the airport was below minimums at arrival time and the aircraft still landed on 4 of them. The 28 afternoon-era Sundays never met fog at 14:00, which is the whole reason the evening-only rate above is the one to plan on.

3. Diverted passengers overnight. No airline transport is promised or documented. On 2 of the 3 observed Medford diversions the aircraft flew Medford → Crescent City the next morning, landing about 10:30–10:45; on the third it went Medford → Oakland → Crescent City, landing at 13:35 Monday. Reaching Crescent City before 8 a.m. after a diversion means driving yourself from Medford (about 2 h 15 min) that night.

4. Calibrated Friday-afternoon risk. Among 59 Sundays in 2025–2026 with an out-of-sample Friday forecast: forecast green 1 of 37 did not land (2.7%, interval 0.3–11.9%), yellow 0 of 5, red 4 of 17 (23.5%, interval 8.5–46.7%). Translating to "not in Crescent City by Monday morning" needs an assumption about overnight recovery that cannot be observed; the defensible range for a green weekend is 1–3.5%, for a red weekend 6–21%.

5. Weekends per year. Flying every weekend: 52 weekends, about 4 to 5 non-landing Sunday evenings a year (4.0 from the evening-only rate, 4.8 weighting by forecast category). Green-only: about 30 weekends, about 1 non-landing evening a year. Green-only from October to May and none in summer: about 26 weekends and under 1. Details and the intervals are in §5.

What the weekday record does for the Friday forecast. It adds no forecast skill (same models, same lead), but it tests the colour categories on 257 more evenings that had an archived out-of-sample Friday forecast (2025–2026). Green: 5 of 151 did not land, none in weather (all clear-air no-service); below-minimums weather never occurred on a green evening. Yellow: 0 of 39. Red: 5 of 67 (7.5%), 4 of them in weather; below-minimums conditions occurred on 14 of the 67. Pooled with the Sundays, the clear-air no-service floor is 15 of 461 evenings (3.3%, 1.9–5.2), tighter than the Sunday-only estimate, and a green forecast has produced no weather failure in 188 evenings. The red category is where Sunday and weekday differ: 4 of 17 Sundays against 5 of 67 weekday evenings. The later Sunday arrival is the obvious suspect, and the Sunday red rate stands as the planning number; the weekday figure says it is unlikely to be an underestimate.

Three things stand out. Every diversion happened on a red-forecast Sunday. The one green failure (25 Jan 2026) was a clear-weather no-service evening, which no weather model can see. And "below minimums" on the METAR is not a legal stop: the published decision altitude is 250 ft above the runway with ¾ mile visibility (½ mile with flight director or autopilot), and Part 135.225 forbids starting the final approach only when the reported visibility is below the minimum. A reported overcast at 200 ft with 1 mile visibility is legal to attempt, and the crews did land through it on 4 of 9 such Sundays. The one quarter-mile fog evening (1 Feb 2026) ended in Medford.

3. What Advanced Air does when it cannot land

  • Medford is the standing alternate. The monthly activity report has permanent line items "Enplaned/Deplaned in MFR due to Diversion", and all three reconstructed diversions landed at Medford. Arcata was used once (Dec 2024, per the airline's report). Confirmed
  • No ground-transport commitment. Contract of Carriage (10 Oct 2025) §29: "Ground Transportation is exclusively the responsibility of the Passenger." §24(a): if the carrier cancels or fails to operate a flight it will, at the passenger's request, refund in full or rebook on another carrier up to $150 or the ticket price. Weather is a defined Force Majeure Event; no hotel or meal provision exists. The Public Charter Operator-Participant Agreement (Jan 2025) likewise limits remedies to refund. In Jan 2024 the airport authority said Advanced Air was "looking into" a Medford arrangement but had not committed. Confirmed
  • What the aircraft actually did after diverting. 1 Feb 2026: flight ran 3.5 h late, missed at 21:45 in ¼-mile fog, Medford 22:26, flew to Crescent City 10:24 next morning. 5 Jul 2026: the Hawthorne arrival missed at 18:30, Medford 19:16; the 21:18 Oakland flight that night did not operate; Monday the aircraft went Medford → Oakland → Crescent City (landed 13:35). 19 Jul 2026: missed twice at 22:00, Medford 22:39, Crescent City 10:30 Monday. Confirmed So passengers who stay with the airline reach Crescent City late Monday morning or early afternoon; the only way to be at work at 8 a.m. is a rental car or ride from Medford that night. How often passengers do that is unobservable. Unknown
  • Pre-departure weather cancellations. The airline's reports log weather as "uncontrollable delays" (0–6 per month), not cancellations, and its agreement pays nothing for flights "cancelled in advance", so the incentive is to attempt or hold. Two of the five no-service Sundays were fog evenings (24 Aug 2025, 14 Jun 2026); whether those were called before departure is not determinable. Inferred
  • Stranded in Oakland. No return-to-Oakland after an approach was observed in 131 Sundays. It does happen: on Saturday 3 Oct 2026 the aircraft missed at Crescent City in ¼-mile fog and flew back to Oakland (the one case in 204 reconstructed evenings). On Sundays, being left in Oakland happens through no-service evenings (4.2%), which are mostly mechanical and announced from Oakland.

Two inquiries are drafted in reports/inquiries.md: one to Advanced Air (bookings@flyadvancedair.com) on diversion practice and ground transport, one to the airport director (s.rosenthal@co.del-norte.ca.us) as a Public Records Act request for every monthly activity report since March 2024. The reports already published in packets show the request is routine.

4. Audit of the fog model

  • Availability of predictors at issue time. The archived forecasts come from Open-Meteo's previous-runs API, whose definition is "the value predicted 24 × N hours before valid time". For a Sunday 18:42 PDT arrival (01:42 UTC Monday), previous_day3 is the run initialised by 01:42 UTC Friday, i.e. the Friday 00 UTC cycle, public by about 01:00 PDT Friday. A Friday 15:00 decision could in fact use the fresher 12 UTC cycle, so the backtest is conservative, not leaky. The Thursday check uses previous_day4 (Thursday 00 UTC run), same logic. TAF and METAR were used only at or after their issue times. The season terms use calendar month only. Confirmed
  • Reproduction with intervals. Quarterly rolling-origin validation, 1 Jan 2025 – 17 Oct 2026, 440 evenings, 34 below minimums. Nested model at the Friday lead: AUC 0.897, bootstrap 95% interval 0.852–0.935 (climatology 0.685); Brier skill versus monthly climatology +18.7%, interval +1.7% to +32.0%. Thursday lead: AUC 0.888 (0.837–0.931), skill +18.8% (6.2–28.2%). The earlier figures (0.89 and +18%) reproduce; the skill-score interval is wide because 34 events is few.
  • The nesting bug. The 25 Oct example (P marginal 0.12, P below 0.14) came from two independently fitted logistic models. Fixed: P(below) is now P(marginal-or-worse) × P(below | marginal-or-worse), fitted on the marginal subset, so the probabilities cannot cross. The live script now gives 0.12 and 0.09 for that date. Calibration of the nested probability is good up to 0.3 and over-forecasts in the top bin (0.60 predicted, 0.41 observed, n = 29).
  • Minimums versus thresholds. The model's targets are weather thresholds (ceiling below 300 ft or visibility below ¾ mile; marginal at 500 ft / 1.5 mile), chosen because METAR ceilings come in 100 ft steps. They are not the legal test. The legal test is reported visibility against ¾ mile (½ with FD/AP), and §2 shows the crews landing through 200 ft ceilings when visibility allowed. The decision rule therefore keys on P(marginal-or-worse), which is what the forecast resolves, and the empirical outcome rates in §2 are what calibrate it to real failures.
  • Green-weekend failure rate with intervals. Weather proxy, all days 2025–26: green 2 of 269 below minimums (0.7%, 0.2–2.4%). Sundays Oct–May: 0 of 33 (upper bound 7.3%). Real outcomes, green Sundays: 1 of 37 did not land (2.7%, 0.3–11.9%), and that one was mechanical. Honest summary: a green weekend's failure rate is a few percent, bounded above near 10%, dominated by non-weather causes.

5. NBM, ADS-B, and what to stop doing

NBM. 2,332 daily 12 UTC National Blend bulletins for KCEC (May 2020 – Oct 2026) were pulled from NOAA's open bucket and parsed for ceiling, visibility and the IFR probabilities. Alone, the IFR-ceiling probability discriminates below-minimums evenings with AUC 0.82 at 14 h, 0.74 at 38 h and 0.73 at 62 h. Added to the four-model ensemble at the Friday lead it changes nothing (AUC 0.882 with versus 0.892 without, same Brier score; n = 251 overlap days). Keep it as a Sunday-morning sanity check, drop it from the Friday model. Confirmed

HRRR direct ceiling and visibility (tested 20 Oct 2026). The 3 km HRRR model forecasts ceiling and visibility explicitly, out to 48 h, and its archive on AWS runs from Dec 2020. Point forecasts for the airport were pulled for every day (2,117 days, 391 marginal-or-worse evenings, 176 below minimums) from the Saturday 00Z run (issued Friday 17:00 PDT, hours 47–48) and the Saturday 06Z run (issued Saturday 01:00, hours 44–45), and scored with the same rolling validation as the ensemble. Result: HRRR alone AUC 0.78 for marginal-or-worse against the ensemble's 0.87, and 0.77–0.80 for below minimums against 0.90. Stacked on top of the ensemble it changed nothing (0.867 vs 0.869). As a raw rule, "HRRR says below minimums" catches 60–66% of below-minimums evenings at 26% precision. Over the full 2022–2026 archive HRRR's skill is stable (AUC 0.78, 1,731 evenings), so this is not a small-sample fluke. Confirmed Conclusion: the forecast fields that name fog directly are worse predictors of fog at this airport than humidity, dewpoint spread and wind from the global ensemble. Files: code/analysis/hrrr_pull.py, hrrr_eval.py, data/hrrr_eval_results.txt.

Live receiver probe (20 Oct 2026). OpenSky, adsb.lol and adsb.fi were polled every 20 s around the airport through that evening's arrival. Two networks tracked the Dornier to 8,150 ft about 25 km out at 18:18 and then lost it; OpenSky never saw it; no aircraft of any kind appeared below 3,000 ft on any feed. OpenSky's historical database would therefore not add landing evidence. Confirmed

ADS-B. Works, with the caveats in §1: all 136 Sundays were processed, 131 got a determinate outcome, 5 did not, and the "attempted approach" question is answerable only through the short-gap signature. The 2024 files also corrected the schedule history (afternoon flight through Sep 2024), which the published timetable alone could not. The weekday extension (every evening flight Oct 2024 – Oct 2026, 467 evenings, 438 determinate) ran unchanged and is reported in §2. Building it required pulling 531 daily archives (about 1.8 TB streamed) and exposed two archive-quality problems that the Sunday run had not: dropped download streams that silently lost part of a day (found by a continuity test at the UTC day boundary and re-pulled), and days where the archive itself is thin. Both now flag a day as incomplete rather than letting an absent aircraft read as a cancelled flight. If more precision is wanted, the next-best sources are, in order: the airline's monthly reports via the records request (exact counts, all days, no inference), and FlightAware AeroAPI history (paid, about 3–8 months depth).

6. Passenger-arrival model

Outcomes per Sunday: A landed that evening; B diverted; C no service; D not in Crescent City by Monday morning. D is not observed, so P(D | category) = P(B | category) × (1 − pB) + P(C | category) × (1 − pC), where pB is the chance a diverted passenger gets from Medford to Crescent City overnight and pC the chance a passenger with a cancelled flight gets there by other means (a 6.5–7 h drive from Oakland, feasible only if the cancellation is known by mid-afternoon). Reading of the tradeoff. Yellow adds eight weekends a year for about 0.3 extra bad evenings; the data cannot distinguish yellow from green (0 of 5). Red weekends are a one-in-four proposition in summer and one-in-seven in winter, with the diversion ending in Medford at 10 p.m. If a missed Monday costs more than roughly ten skipped weekends, fly green and yellow; otherwise fly green only. In either case the Sunday-morning re-check matters most on yellow.
